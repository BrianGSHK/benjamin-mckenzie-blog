Early Life and Education

Benjamin McKenzie grew up in Fraserburgh, a small town on Scotland’s north-east coast. He remembers Fraserburgh Harbour Lighthouse and the old days playing football at Bellslea Park. Those local experiences gave him an early appreciation for community, persistence, and practical problem-solving, while also encouraging his interest in how businesses operate and grow.

His academic journey led him to the University of Cambridge, one of the world's most prestigious institutions, where he completed a BA in Economics and gained a rigorous foundation in economic analysis, finance, and strategic thinking. At Cambridge, McKenzie honed the analytical skills and strategic thinking that would later define his approach to mentoring startups. The university's rich tradition of fostering innovation and critical thinking gave him the intellectual framework to understand complex business structures and financial systems — skills that would prove invaluable in his future career.

Move to Hong Kong

After completing his studies at Cambridge, McKenzie made the pivotal decision to relocate to Hong Kong. Drawn by the city's position as a global financial hub and its gateway role to Asian markets, he recognized the immense opportunities the region offered for professionals with his background. Hong Kong's dynamic business ecosystem, combined with its proximity to rapidly growing markets across Asia, made it the ideal base for building a career at the intersection of finance and entrepreneurship.

The move to Hong Kong marked the beginning of a decade-long immersion in one of the world's most vibrant business environments. McKenzie quickly established himself within the city's tight-knit financial community, building relationships and developing deep expertise in the regulatory and commercial landscape of the region.

Career in Venture Capital and Private Equity

Over the course of more than a decade, Benjamin McKenzie built a distinguished career in venture capital and private equity in Hong Kong. Working with prominent firms in the region, he evaluated hundreds of startups and growth-stage companies across Asia, from early-stage technology ventures to established businesses seeking expansion capital.

His work in VC and PE gave him a panoramic view of what makes businesses succeed or fail. He saw firsthand how promising companies could stumble due to avoidable mistakes in their foundational structures — incorrect entity types, poor compliance practices, inadequate financial planning, and misaligned business structures. These observations planted the seed for his future work as a mentor and advisor.

During his time in venture capital, McKenzie developed a deep understanding of what investors look for in a company's foundations. He learned that a well-structured company with proper compliance from day one is far more attractive to investors and far less likely to face costly remediation later. This insight became a cornerstone of his mentoring philosophy.

Transition to Startup Mentoring

Having seen the patterns that lead to both success and failure, McKenzie transitioned from institutional investing to direct mentorship. He realized he could make a more meaningful impact by helping founders get their foundations right from the very beginning, rather than evaluating them after the fact.

Today, as a Startup Mentor based in Hong Kong, Benjamin McKenzie focuses on demystifying company formation, compliance, and business setup for entrepreneurs. His approach is grounded in practical, actionable guidance rather than theoretical advice. He helps founders navigate the complexities of Hong Kong company registration, understand their annual compliance obligations, and make informed decisions about business structures that align with their goals.

Through this blog, he shares his expertise with a global audience of entrepreneurs and founders. His articles cover topics ranging from the practical steps of registering a limited company as a non-resident to opening bank accounts and understanding offshore vs onshore structures.

Philosophy and Approach

Benjamin McKenzie's approach to mentoring is shaped by his years in venture capital. He believes that the strongest companies are built on solid foundations, and that founders who take the time to get their entity structures and compliance right early on are far more likely to succeed in the long run.

His core philosophy centers on three principles: clarity over complexity, founder-first thinking, and radical transparency. He breaks down complicated regulatory frameworks into clear, actionable guidance. He tailors his advice to each founder's unique situation, industry, and goals. And he communicates openly and honestly, ensuring founders understand not just what to do, but why.

Whether he is writing detailed guides on registering a company in Hong Kong, comparing company formation services, or breaking down startup costs, McKenzie's work reflects his commitment to empowering entrepreneurs with the knowledge they need to build with confidence.
