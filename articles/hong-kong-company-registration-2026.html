How to Register a Hong Kong Company in 2026: Complete Guide

A step-by-step walkthrough of the registration process, from choosing your business structure to opening a bank account.

Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Founder tools: Start with the HK company formation timeline and TCSP licensing guide.

Hong Kong remains one of the most attractive jurisdictions for company formation. With a straightforward registration process and a territorial tax system, subject to applicable legal and tax conditions, it's no surprise that thousands of entrepreneurs choose Hong Kong every year.

This guide walks you through the entire process — from initial planning to your first bank transaction. The official Companies Registry guidance is the reference point for statutory incorporation requirements.

Key Takeaway: Registering a Hong Kong company can be administratively straightforward, but incorporation is only the first step. Non-resident founders still need proper identity verification, a licensed company-service provider where applicable, a credible registered address and a defensible KYC record. For costs, see our complete cost breakdown; for the next stage, read the Hong Kong bank-account guide.

This guide explains the registration process in practical terms. For a professional tax overview after incorporation, see PwC Hong Kong’s corporate tax guidance, which discusses profits tax obligations for companies carrying on business in Hong Kong.

Direct Answers

How do you register a Hong Kong company in 2026?

A Hong Kong company is registered by choosing a compliant name, appointing directors, shareholders and a company secretary, securing a registered office, preparing the incorporation documents, and filing with the Companies Registry. For non-residents, electronic filing may be coordinated by an agent or eligible professional, but identity verification and banking due diligence remain separate steps.

How long does Hong Kong company registration take?

Standard Hong Kong company incorporation commonly takes one to three business days after the Companies Registry receives complete and accurate documents. The timeline can extend when identity checks, company-name issues, document corrections, or additional compliance questions arise. Opening a corporate bank account normally requires separate due diligence and may take longer.

How much does it cost to form a Hong Kong company?

First-year formation costs commonly range from HK$5,000 to HK$15,000 when professional services include the government filing, registered office, and company secretary. Government fees and business-registration charges can change, while banking, accounting, audit, and tax services may create additional costs depending on the company’s activities.

Why Hong Kong?

Before diving into the registration process, it's worth understanding why Hong Kong remains a top choice for international business:

  • Foreign ownership is generally permitted — Subject to the Companies Ordinance and any sector-specific rules
  • Low tax burden — 8.25% on the first HK$2 million of profits; 16.5% thereafter (see the Inland Revenue Department profits tax guidance)
  • Foreign-sourced profits may receive different tax treatment — The result depends on the facts, source rules, and applicable exemptions
  • Strategic location — Gateway to Mainland China and Southeast Asian markets
  • Rule of law — Transparent legal system based on English common law

Step 1: Choose Your Business Structure

Most foreign entrepreneurs choose one of two structures:

Structure Limited Liability Minimum Requirements Best For
Private Limited Company Yes 1 director, 1 shareholder, 1 company secretary Most businesses
Sole Proprietorship No 1 owner, business registration only Freelancers, consultants

Practical view: For many foreign entrepreneurs, a Private Limited Company is a common choice, It provides limited liability protection, looks more professional to clients and banks, and is required for certain business activities.

Step 2: Prepare Your Documents

Gather the following documents before starting the registration process:

  1. Company name — Must end with "Limited" or "Ltd." Check availability on the Companies Registry website
  2. Registered address — Must be a physical address in Hong Kong (not a P.O. box)
  3. Director details — At least one director; can be any nationality; no residency requirement
  4. Shareholder details — Can be individuals or corporate entities; can hold shares in any proportion
  5. Company secretary — Must be a Hong Kong resident or a Hong Kong-registered company; see the Companies Registry's director and secretary requirements
  6. Articles of Association — Governs internal management; can use the standard model

Step 3: Submit Your Application

The application is submitted to the Companies Registry via the official company-registration service and its "e-Registry" portal. The Registry's incorporation FAQ sets out the required forms and filing process. Processing times:

  • Online application: 1-3 business days
  • Paper application: 4-7 business days

Once approved, you'll receive your Certificate of Incorporation and Business Registration Certificate.

Step 4: Open a Bank Account

This is often the most challenging step. Hong Kong banks have increased their due diligence requirements in recent years. For a detailed guide on opening a bank account as a foreigner, see our Hong Kong Bank Account Guide.

Tip: Prepare a detailed business plan, proof of business activities (invoices, contracts, website), and personal bank references. Having a company formation agent who maintains a relationship with specific banks can significantly improve your chances.

Documents typically required:

  • Certificate of Incorporation
  • Business Registration Certificate
  • Articles of Association
  • Passport copies of directors and shareholders
  • Proof of address (utility bill, bank statement)
  • Business plan or description of business activities
  • Corporate structure chart (for complex ownership)

How do you verify a Hong Kong company secretary’s TCSP licence?

A company secretary providing company-service activities should be checked against Hong Kong’s official Register of Trust or Company Service Provider Licensees. Search the provider’s exact legal name or licence number, confirm the status and business address, and check the result before signing. A marketing logo or a Companies Registry search alone is not proof of a valid TCSP licence.

  1. Ask for the provider’s exact legal name, Hong Kong registered address and TCSP licence number.
  2. Search the official TCSP public register by name or licence number.
  3. Match the legal name and address against the engagement letter and invoice.
  4. Check the TCSP disciplinary cases page and ask whether any suspension, condition or change affects the proposed service.
  5. Keep a dated copy or PDF of the search result. The Companies Registry’s TCSP licensing guideline explains that the Registrar can discipline, suspend or revoke a licence.

Can a non-resident incorporate a Hong Kong company entirely online?

Non-Hong Kong residents may incorporate a local company, and the Companies Registry provides electronic filing. However, “online filing” does not remove identity and certification requirements. If you are not physically in Hong Kong, you will generally need a properly appointed incorporation agent or eligible professional to coordinate filing, identity verification and document certification. A non-resident may need a certified true copy of a passport or other identification document. Confirm the route with the agent before paying.

The Companies Registry’s e-Services guidance identifies passports for non-HKID holders and lists acceptable certifiers, including a Hong Kong practising solicitor, practising CPA, professional company secretary or notary public. If you are not physically in Hong Kong, a properly qualified incorporation agent can coordinate the filing and certification route; it is not a licence to skip KYC.

What should a good Hong Kong incorporation agent ask before accepting an order?

A responsible agent should conduct genuine KYC and understand the proposed business before incorporation. Expect a real-time video call or equivalent identity check, questions about business activities, expected turnover, target countries, customers, counterparties and source of funds, and a request for supporting documents. An agent that accepts a suspiciously cheap order without questions may create avoidable banking and compliance risk.

These checks do not guarantee bank approval. Banks and e-wallet providers make their own risk decisions and may screen the company, directors, shareholders, counterparties and addresses against sanctions and adverse-information databases. For a practical cross-border check, review the OFAC Sanctions List Search Tool; US sanctions screening is not the only applicable check, but it demonstrates why a registered address and ownership structure should be taken seriously.

What have I seen when mentoring founders through company formation?

In my startup mentoring work, I have supported more than 10,000 startups and founders across different markets. A recurring lesson is that incorporation is the easy part; the harder task is preserving a credible compliance trail for banks, e-wallet providers and future counterparties. I have seen founders surprised by account rejections where they did not understand how the agent, registered address, ownership structure or business description affected onboarding.

One founder I advised recently asked why several banks had declined the company without giving a detailed reason. After seeking general feedback through banking contacts, the concern related to AML screening connected with the registered-address and ownership information. That experience is not proof that every company using an address is problematic, and banking decisions remain confidential and institution-specific. The practical lesson is to select an agent that performs real KYC, keeps clear records and can explain its compliance process before incorporation.

Why can a registered address or weak KYC process create banking problems?

A registered address is not merely a mailing label. A bank may assess whether the address, company-service provider, directors, shareholders and business model fit the customer’s stated activities and risk profile. A prior allegation or sanctions match involving an address does not automatically prove that every company using it is involved, but unresolved matches can trigger enhanced due diligence, rejection or account restrictions.

Do not present an unverified personal anecdote as proof that a particular agent or address is sanctioned. Instead, ask the agent how it checks clients, maintains records and responds to sanctions or adverse-media alerts. Hong Kong’s official AML guidance for money-service businesses highlights risk-based due diligence and sanctions screening of parties involved in payments.

Is an unusually cheap incorporation package a warning sign?

Price alone does not prove poor service, but an unusually cheap package may exclude the registered office, company secretary, KYC work, document certification, annual return, business-registration charges or post-incorporation support. Compare the full first-year and renewal cost, ask who performs each compliance step, and confirm the provider’s licence and engagement scope before treating a low headline price as a bargain.
