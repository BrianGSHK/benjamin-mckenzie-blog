How to Register a Hong Kong Company in 2026: Complete Guide

A step-by-step walkthrough of the registration process, from choosing your business structure to opening a bank account.

Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Hong Kong remains one of the most attractive jurisdictions for company formation. With a straightforward registration process, no restrictions on foreign ownership, and a territorial tax system, it's no surprise that thousands of entrepreneurs choose Hong Kong every year.

This guide walks you through the entire process — from initial planning to your first bank transaction.

Key Takeaway: Hong Kong company registration takes 1-3 business days. Foreign entrepreneurs can complete the process remotely. Basic setup costs range from HK$5,000 to HK$15,000. You need a registered address and company secretary in Hong Kong.

Why Hong Kong?

Before diving into the registration process, it's worth understanding why Hong Kong remains a top choice for international business:

  • 100% foreign ownership — No restrictions on foreign directors or shareholders
  • Low tax burden — 8.25% on the first HK$2 million of profits; 16.5% thereafter
  • Offshore tax exemption — Active trading profits earned outside Hong Kong can be tax-free
  • Strategic location — Gateway to Mainland China and Southeast Asian markets
  • Rule of law — Transparent legal system based on English common law

Step 1: Choose Your Business Structure

Most foreign entrepreneurs choose one of two structures:

Structure Limited Liability Minimum Requirements Best For
Private Limited Company Yes 1 director, 1 shareholder, 1 company secretary Most businesses
Sole Proprietorship No 1 owner, business registration only Freelancers, consultants

Recommendation: For most foreign entrepreneurs, a Private Limited Company is the best choice. It provides limited liability protection, looks more professional to clients and banks, and is required for certain business activities.

Step 2: Prepare Your Documents

Gather the following documents before starting the registration process:

  1. Company name — Must end with "Limited" or "Ltd." Check availability on the Companies Registry website
  2. Registered address — Must be a physical address in Hong Kong (not a P.O. box)
  3. Director details — At least one director; can be any nationality; no residency requirement
  4. Shareholder details — Can be individuals or corporate entities; can hold shares in any proportion
  5. Company secretary — Must be a Hong Kong resident or a Hong Kong-registered company
  6. Articles of Association — Governs internal management; can use the standard model

Step 3: Submit Your Application

The application is submitted to the Companies Registry via the "e-Registry" portal. Processing times:

  • Online application: 1-3 business days
  • Paper application: 4-7 business days

Once approved, you'll receive your Certificate of Incorporation and Business Registration Certificate.

Step 4: Open a Bank Account

This is often the most challenging step. Hong Kong banks have increased their due diligence requirements in recent years.

Tip: Prepare a detailed business plan, proof of business activities (invoices, contracts, website), and personal bank references. Having a company formation agent who maintains a relationship with specific banks can significantly improve your chances.

Documents typically required:

  • Certificate of Incorporation
  • Business Registration Certificate
  • Articles of Association
  • Passport copies of directors and shareholders
  • Proof of address (utility bill, bank statement)
  • Business plan or description of business activities
  • Corporate structure chart (for complex ownership)

Step 5: Ongoing Compliance

After registration, your company must meet these annual requirements:

Obligation Deadline Authority
Annual Return Within 42 days of anniversary Companies Registry
Business Registration Renewal 1 month before expiry Inland Revenue Department
Profits Tax Return As per IRD letter (usually April) Inland Revenue Department
Audited Financial Statements Within 3 months of fiscal year-end Hong Kong-registered auditor
