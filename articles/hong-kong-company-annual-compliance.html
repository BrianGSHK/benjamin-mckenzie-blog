Hong Kong Company Annual Compliance: What You Need to Know

A complete guide to keeping your Hong Kong company in good standing, covering annual returns, audits, tax filing, and deadlines.

Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Hong Kong companies must file an Annual Return within 42 days of their anniversary date, renew their Business Registration Certificate annually, and submit audited financial statements within 3 months of their fiscal year-end. Missing these deadlines results in escalating penalties and potential legal action. If you havent registered your company yet, see our HK Company Registration Guide.

Quick Answer: Annual compliance includes 4 main obligations: Annual Return (HK$105 online, within 42 days), Business Registration Renewal (HK$2,150, 1 month before expiry), Audited Accounts (within 3 months of year-end), and Profits Tax Return (as per IRD schedule).

For an additional professional reference on current filing developments, see KPMG’s 2025/26 Hong Kong Profits Tax filing guide. It should be read alongside the statutory deadlines and notices issued by the Inland Revenue Department.

EY also maintains a Hong Kong tax alert on the 2025/26 Profits Tax filing season, useful for monitoring filing-season developments.

Direct Answers

What are the main annual compliance requirements for a Hong Kong company?

A Hong Kong company generally needs to file its Annual Return, renew its Business Registration Certificate, maintain accounting records, prepare audited financial statements where required, and submit Profits Tax Returns according to the Inland Revenue Department’s timetable. Exact obligations depend on the company’s status, financial year, activity, and any applicable exemptions.

When must a Hong Kong company file its Annual Return?

A private Hong Kong company normally files its Annual Return with the Companies Registry within 42 days after the anniversary of incorporation. The filing confirms current corporate information, including directors, shareholders, registered office, and company secretary. Late filing attracts escalating statutory fees and can create additional enforcement or restoration risks.

Do Hong Kong companies need annual audits?

Hong Kong companies generally need annual financial statements audited by a Hong Kong certified public accountant unless a specific statutory exemption applies. Dormant status or other limited circumstances may affect the requirement, but companies must still review their facts carefully and continue other filings, including Annual Return and business-registration obligations.

Annual Return Filing

The Annual Return is a snapshot of your company's information as of the anniversary date. It must be filed within 42 days of your company's incorporation anniversary. The Companies Registry's private-company annual return guidance explains the filing requirement and late fees. See the official Companies Ordinance reference for the statutory framework.

What Information Is Required?

  • Company name and registration number
  • Registered address
  • Details of directors and company secretary
  • Details of shareholders and their shareholdings
  • Details of any charges on company property

Penalty for Late Filing: Escalating fines — HK$300 (up to 42 days late), HK$870 (42-90 days late), HK$1,740 (over 90 days late). Persistent non-compliance can lead to prosecution and the company being struck off the register.

Business Registration Renewal

Your Business Registration Certificate (BRC) must be renewed annually. The Inland Revenue Department explains the Business Registration renewal process. You can choose:

  • 1-year certificate: HK$2,150
  • 3-year certificate: HK$5,650 (saves approximately HK$800)

The renewal is due one month before the certificate expiry date. Late renewal incurs penalties of HK$300–500.

Audit Requirements

All Hong Kong limited companies must have their financial statements audited by a Hong Kong-registered auditor. This is a legal requirement under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

Audit Process

  1. Prepare financial records — Maintain proper books of account throughout the year
  2. Engage an auditor — Must be a Certified Public Accountant (CPA) registered in Hong Kong
  3. Audit examination — Auditor reviews financial statements and supporting documents
  4. Issue audit report — Auditor issues an opinion on whether statements present a true and fair view
  5. File with IRD — Submit audited accounts with your Profits Tax Return

Timeline

  • Audit must be completed within 3 months of your fiscal year-end
  • If your year-end is March 31, audit must be completed by June 30

Dormant Companies: Companies with no significant accounting transactions during a period may apply for audit exemption under Section 406 of the Companies Ordinance. However, they must still file annual returns and business registration renewals.

Profits Tax Return

Hong Kong operates a territorial tax system — only profits sourced in Hong Kong are taxable. The Profits Tax Return is issued annually by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD). For current filing information, consult the IRD's profits tax guidance.

Key Facts:

  • Tax rates: 8.25% on the first HK$2 million of assessable profits; 16.5% thereafter
  • Filing deadline: Usually April 1st (for companies with March 31 year-end)
  • Response deadline: Typically 1-3 months from the date of issue
  • Offshore exemption: Profits earned outside Hong Kong may be exempt from tax (learn more in our Offshore vs Onshore guide)

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Failing to meet compliance deadlines can result in:

  • Financial penalties — Escalating fines for late filing
  • Legal prosecution — Directors can be personally liable
  • Company strike-off — Companies Registry can remove your company from the register
  • Bank account closure — Banks may freeze or close accounts for non-compliant companies
  • Reputational damage — Non-compliance is a matter of public record
