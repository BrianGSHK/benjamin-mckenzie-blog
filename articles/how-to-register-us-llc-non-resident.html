How to Register a US LLC as a Non-Resident (2026)

A complete guide to forming a US LLC as a non-resident, including state comparisons, costs, requirements, and banking options.

BM
Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 11, 2026 · 10 min read

Non-residents can form and own a US LLC in most states without visiting the United States. Wyoming, Delaware, and Florida are the most popular states for non-resident LLCs due to their business-friendly laws, low fees, and strong privacy protections.

Quick Answer: Wyoming is often the best choice for non-residents — $100 filing fee, no state income tax, strong privacy, and no annual report requirement for single-member LLCs. Total setup cost: $200–500 with a formation service.

Why Form a US LLC as a Non-Resident?

A US LLC provides several advantages for international entrepreneurs:

  • Access to US market — Sell to US customers and accept US payments
  • US bank account — Open a US bank account for dollar transactions
  • Credibility — US business entity looks professional to clients
  • Payment processors — Access to Stripe, PayPal, and other US payment systems
  • Limited liability — Protect personal assets from business debts
  • Flexible taxation — Single-member LLC can be treated as disregarded entity

Wyoming: The Non-Resident Favorite

Wyoming is the most popular choice for non-resident LLCs because:

  • Lowest fees — $100 filing fee, $50 annual fee (or $0 for single-member LLCs with no Wyoming income)
  • No state income tax — No tax on out-of-state or international income
  • Strong privacy — No public disclosure of members or managers
  • No annual report — Single-member LLCs with no Wyoming income don't need to file annual reports
  • Asset protection — Strong charging order protection for LLC members

Delaware: The Investor Favorite

Delaware is preferred by startups seeking venture capital because:

  • Business-friendly courts — Specialized Court of Chancery for business disputes
  • Investor familiarity — VCs and investors are comfortable with Delaware LLCs
  • Flexible LLC laws — Detailed statutory framework for LLC operations

Recommendation: If you're a non-resident not seeking US investment, choose Wyoming. If you plan to raise VC funding, choose Delaware. If you're selling physical products to US customers, consider Florida.

Step-by-Step Registration Process

Step 1: Choose Your State and Company Name

  • Check name availability on the state's Secretary of State website
  • Name must include "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company"
  • Cannot include restricted words (Bank, Insurance, etc.)

Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

A registered agent is required in the state of formation. This is a person or company that receives legal documents on behalf of your LLC. Most formation services offer registered agent service for $50–200/year.

Step 3: File Articles of Organization

Submit the formation document to the state. Most states allow online filing:

  • Wyoming: File online at wyoming SOS website — $100
  • Delaware: File online at Delaware SOS website — $90
  • Florida: File online at Sunbiz website — $125

Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

While not required by most states, an Operating Agreement is essential for:

  • Defining ownership percentages
  • Establishing management structure
  • Outlining profit distribution
  • Setting rules for adding/removing members

Step 5: Obtain an EIN (Employer Identification Number)

An EIN is like a Social Security Number for your business. Non-residents can:

  • Apply online — If you have an SSN or ITIN
  • Apply by fax — Using Form SS-4 (no SSN required)
  • Apply by mail — Using Form SS-4 (takes 4-6 weeks)

Tip: The EIN is necessary for opening a US bank account, hiring employees, and filing taxes. Apply for it immediately after forming your LLC.

Step 6: Open a US Bank Account

This is often the most challenging step for non-residents. Options include:

  • Mercury — Popular with startups, fully remote, no minimum balance
  • Brex — Business credit card + bank account, no personal guarantee
  • Relay — Digital banking for businesses, free to use
  • Wise Business — Multi-currency account with US details
  • Traditional banks — May require in-person visit (difficult for non-residents)

Tax Implications for Non-Resident LLCs

Non-resident LLCs have specific tax obligations:

Federal Taxes

  • Single-member LLC — Treated as disregarded entity; income reported on owner's personal return
  • Multi-member LLC — Treated as partnership; files Form 1065
  • Election to be taxed as corporation — Can elect C-corp or S-corp treatment

State Taxes

  • Wyoming: No state income tax
  • Delaware: No tax on out-of-state income; franchise tax if operating in Delaware
  • Florida: 5.5% corporate income tax (if operating in Florida)

Important: Non-residents with US-sourced income may need to file a US tax return. Consult a tax professional to determine your specific obligations.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Choosing the wrong state — Don't automatically choose Delaware; Wyoming may be better for non-residents
  • Skipping the Operating Agreement — This document protects you and defines how the LLC operates
  • Ignoring tax obligations — Even if you don't owe taxes, you may need to file returns
  • Using a personal bank account — Always use a separate business bank account
  • Forgetting annual fees — Most states require annual reports and fees to keep your LLC in good standing
