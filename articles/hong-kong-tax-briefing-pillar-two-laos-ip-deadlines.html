Hong Kong Tax Briefing: Pillar Two E-Filing Goes Live, New Laos CDTA Signed, and Enforcement Signals Tough Stance on Fraud.

Four developments touch very different companies. Here is how to tell which applies to your startup, what to prepare, and which dates matter.

By Benjamin McKenzie · Last reviewed: 3 November 2026 · General information, not tax advice

Editorial translation of Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department guidance — not HKICPA-registered tax advice. The IRD assesses each taxpayer on the facts of the year of assessment; confirm with a HKICPA-registered adviser before relying on any figure, formula or threshold in this guide.

Short answer: Most early-stage companies will not be subject to Pillar Two, and the signed Laos tax agreement is not yet in force. But the 2025/26 “M” Code return deadlines apply to eligible filing arrangements now; the IP deduction proposals remain a Bill; and all employers should be able to reconcile wages claimed with real work and real payments.

Founder action list
  1. Ask your group finance lead whether the €750 million Pillar Two threshold is met; do not infer scope from having a Hong Kong company.
  2. Check the accounting date code and filing method on your 2025/26 Profits Tax Return; the IRD’s amended M-code dates are 30 November (paper) and 30 December (electronic). If applying for an M-code loss-case extension, note that the general list deadline has passed, although exceptional individual requests may be lodged by 16 November.
  3. If your business buys, transfers or licenses IP, preserve the signed contract, valuation and commercial rationale—but do not claim a proposed deduction as current law.
  4. For Laos payments, use current domestic rules until the treaty enters into force and confirm its conditions for the particular payment.

Does Pillar Two apply to your Hong Kong startup?

Usually not if you are a standalone startup or a smaller group. Hong Kong’s Pillar Two minimum-tax regime generally targets a multinational enterprise (MNE) group with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million in at least two of the four fiscal years immediately before the current year. A Hong Kong incorporation by itself does not put a company in scope.

If your startup is part of a larger multinational group, the parent’s consolidated accounts and group structure—not your local entity’s turnover alone—are the starting point.

For in-scope groups, Hong Kong’s rules include the Income Inclusion Rule and Hong Kong Minimum Top-up Tax (HKMTT), designed around a 15% minimum effective tax rate. This is a specialised, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction calculation—not a simple 15% charge on a Hong Kong subsidiary’s accounting profit. Have the group tax team establish scope and identify the responsible Hong Kong filing entity. The IRD Pillar Two guidance sets out the threshold and mechanics.

On 26 October 2026, the IRD added BIR82 top-up tax return filing and assessment-notice functions to the Pillar Two Portal. BIR82 is separate from an ordinary Profits Tax Return such as BIR51. Its usual deadline is 15 months after fiscal year-end, or 18 months for the group’s first transition year. A separate top-up tax notification is generally due within six months. The IRD also allows one designated local entity to file for other Hong Kong constituent entities in specified circumstances; confirm the group’s filing arrangement rather than assuming every subsidiary submits a separate return.

An entity does not avoid its return obligation merely because its calculation shows no top-up tax due. Check the IRD’s Portal FAQs and user guides for filing roles, access and authentication requirements.

Do this if you are in a group. Confirm in writing which entity is the designated filing entity, who owns the group data, and who can access the Business Tax Portal. Portal transactions require an organisational e-Cert with AEOI functions. Do not assume that an existing tax representative can submit BIR82: the IRD’s FAQ distinguishes a tax representative from an engaged Pillar Two service provider.

What does the Hong Kong–Laos tax treaty change for founders?

Hong Kong and Laos signed a comprehensive double taxation agreement (CDTA) on 29 October 2026. It allocates taxing rights and provides relief from double taxation, but it is not yet in force. Both governments must complete ratification procedures.

Until then, signing alone does not entitle your company to apply the treaty’s lower withholding-tax limits. Check the Government announcement and the signed agreement text again before making a cross-border payment.

Once effective, the text caps source-state tax on interest and royalties at 5%, subject to treaty conditions. Dividends have an 8% general cap. A 5% cap applies to a qualifying corporate beneficial owner that directly holds at least 15% of the payer’s capital throughout a 365-day period including the payment date.

These are treaty ceilings, not automatic exemptions. Beneficial ownership and permanent-establishment connections can change the result.

If you send staff to Laos or deliver a project there, permanent-establishment rules also matter. The agreement includes a construction or installation threshold of more than six months and a services threshold of more than 183 days in a 12-month period for activities through employees or other personnel.

Those thresholds do not replace fixed-place and dependent-agent tests. Before launch, map where people work, who signs contracts, what they do and how long the project lasts. Ask a cross-border adviser to review the exact model once the treaty takes effect.

What does the wage-expense prosecution mean for your payroll?

On 26 October, a businessman was sentenced to five months’ imprisonment and fined HK$60,000 after pleading guilty to four tax-evasion charges. The IRD reported HK$803,000 in false wage-expense claims and HK$98,858 in tax involved. The case concerned false wage claims, a false Employer’s Return and wages for fictitious workers; it is an enforcement example, not evidence that every payroll mistake is fraud.

The claims included wages for fictitious workers and a false Employer’s Return. The prosecution was under sections 82(1)(c), (d) and (g) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Read the IRD case release for the specific facts.

This one case does not establish that every payroll error is fraud or prove that authorities use any particular data-matching system. The practical point for an owner-manager is narrower: wage deductions and employer filings should be supported by people who actually worked and payments you can reconcile.

Is the IP tax deduction Bill already law?

No. The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Tax Deductions for Intellectual Property Expenditure) Bill 2026 proposes broader deductions for certain capital spending on intellectual-property rights and licences. It was introduced for First and Second Reading on 28 October; the Second Reading debate was adjourned.

LegCo’s 30 October House Committee agenda listed the Bill for legal-service review. As at 3 November 2026, it remains a Bill, not an enacted deduction; LegCo’s Bills Committee page lists a meeting for 24 November, but the linked bills-committee page can only be independently checked for the page title and the listing is dynamic. Follow the IRD Bill summary, LegCo House Committee agenda and LegCo Bills Committee page for its status.

The proposal covers three founder-relevant situations: eligible IP acquired from associates; certain IP bought for use by a licensee outside Hong Kong where the related income is chargeable to Profits Tax under the foreign-sourced income exemption (FSIE) regime, with a proportionate deduction; and upfront licence fees for eligible rights.

It proposes to apply to qualifying contracts entered into on or after 1 April 2026. Proposed safeguards include a main-purpose test for associate purchases, a prescribed calculation in some Hong Kong associate transfers and independent valuations in specified related-party cases above a threshold. The available official summary does not state the threshold amount, so this article does not guess it.

If you are negotiating an IP transfer or licence, keep the executed agreement, board approval, valuation, related-party analysis, evidence of how the IP will be used and the commercial rationale. Ask your tax adviser to confirm the eventual enacted wording and commencement before including any deduction in a return. Tax deductibility and accounting recognition are separate questions; the proposal does not itself change the FSIE tests.

Which 2025/26 filing date applies to an M-code return?

If the company’s accounting date falls from 1 January to 31 March 2026, its 2025/26 Profits Tax Return has an M accounting-date code. The IRD block-extension scheme is an arrangement for represented taxpayers; the return and the representative’s filing route determine which extension applies. A later IRD circular dated 20 October further extended the relevant M-code filing dates as follows:

The 30 November and 30 December dates come from the Commissioner’s 20 October circular, which further extended the dates set under the 2025/26 block-extension programme. The arrangement is for represented taxpayers; additional rules apply to mandatory e-filers, voluntary e-filers and paper-return exceptions.

A separate loss-case extension may move an eligible M-code return to 1 February 2027. The 2025/26 tax-representative circular requires an allowable loss and sets the general application-list deadline at 2 November 2026. As at 3 November 2026, that general deadline has passed; exceptional individual requests may be lodged on or before 16 November 2026. Confirm with the tax representative and IRD that any request is permitted and acknowledged rather than assuming an extension.

Do not assume the extension applies automatically to every company or use the loss-case route to delay a profitable return.

These Profits Tax Return dates are separate from Pillar Two BIR82 deadlines and from the date printed on a particular notice. Keep the return, accounting code, representative status, filing method and extension acknowledgement together in the company’s compliance folder.

If you are a qualifying Part 4AA entity, the separate one-month mandatory e-filing extension may apply. The IRD circular says it is automatic only where the Department was notified of the entity’s status before March 2026; other cases require an extension request. Voluntary e-filers may apply separately for an additional month, subject to the circular’s conditions.

What should a founder do this week?

  1. Classify your group: if you are part of a multinational group, ask for a documented Pillar Two scope assessment against the €750 million/two-of-four-years test.
  2. Check your return letter: verify the year of assessment, M code, due date, e-filing route and any extension confirmation. If a 2025/26 M-code loss-case list was not submitted by 2 November, ask your tax representative whether an exceptional individual request can still be made by 16 November.
  3. Reconcile payroll: match each employee on the employer return to actual service, payroll calculation and payment evidence.
  4. Preserve IP deal records: save executed contracts, valuations and commercial approvals, but wait for enacted legislation and adviser confirmation before claiming proposed relief.
  5. Revisit Laos plans later: monitor ratification and effective dates; until then, apply current rules and price withholding based on the current position.

Frequently asked questions

Does the new Pillar Two Portal apply to every Hong Kong startup?

No. The 15% global minimum-tax rules generally target multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in at least two of the four fiscal years immediately before the current year. A small standalone startup is not brought into scope just because it has a Hong Kong company.

What changed in the Pillar Two Portal on 26 October 2026?

The Inland Revenue Department added electronic filing of the BIR82 Top-up Tax Return and electronic access to top-up tax assessment notices for qualifying Part 4AA entities in in-scope groups for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025. The BIR82 is separate from an ordinary Profits Tax Return.

Is the Hong Kong–Laos tax treaty already in force?

No. Hong Kong and Laos signed the agreement on 29 October 2026, but it takes effect only after both sides complete ratification procedures. Do not apply its reduced withholding-tax caps to a payment merely because the agreement was signed.

What are the 2025/26 M-code Profits Tax Return deadlines?

For M-code returns with accounting dates from 1 January to 31 March 2026, the Inland Revenue Department extended the paper deadline to 30 November 2026 and the electronic deadline to 30 December 2026 under the 20 October circular. Check the company’s return, filing route and applicable representation arrangements. Qualifying Part 4AA entities and voluntary e-filers may have separate further-extension rules. The ordinary M-code loss-case list deadline was 2 November; exceptional individual requests may be made by 16 November.

Has Hong Kong enacted the 2026 IP tax-deduction Bill?

No. As at 3 November 2026, the Bill had been introduced for First and Second Reading on 28 October, with the Second Reading debate adjourned. LegCo’s 30 October House Committee agenda listed it for legal-service review; its Bills Committee page lists a meeting for 24 November. The proposals are not current law.

What payroll evidence should a founder retain?

Keep employment agreements, employee identity and start-date records, attendance or service evidence appropriate to the role, payroll calculations, bank payment records, payslips and Employer’s Return data, with a clear reconciliation between the records. The exact records depend on the business and applicable duties; do not report wages for a person who did not work or receive the payment.
