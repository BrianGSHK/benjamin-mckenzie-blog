How Much Does It Cost to Start a Business in Hong Kong?

A complete breakdown of all costs involved in forming and maintaining a Hong Kong company, from government fees to ongoing compliance.

Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 11, 2026 · 6 min read

The total cost to start a business in Hong Kong ranges from HK$5,000 to HK$15,000 for the first year. Government charges can change, so check the Inland Revenue Department's current Business Registration fee and levy table before budgeting. This includes government registration fees, company secretary services, and a registered address. For a complete walkthrough, see our HK Company Registration Guide. Ongoing annual costs typically run HK$8,000 to HK$20,000.

Quick Answer: Government fees are approximately HK$1,720. With a formation agent (including registered address and company secretary), expect HK$5,000–15,000 total for the first year. Annual maintenance costs HK$8,000–20,000.

Tax and compliance estimates should be checked against current professional references. PwC publishes a Hong Kong SAR tax summary, while government charges should still be confirmed with the relevant Hong Kong authorities.

Direct Answers

How much money is needed to start a business in Hong Kong?

A typical Hong Kong company formation budget is HK$5,000 to HK$15,000 for the first year when it includes incorporation, a registered address, and company-secretary services. This estimate excludes many operating costs, such as staff, premises, accounting, audit, banking, insurance, licences, and sector-specific compliance requirements.

What are the ongoing annual costs of a Hong Kong company?

Ongoing annual costs commonly range from HK$8,000 to HK$20,000 for a small company using outsourced compliance services. The total may include the Annual Return, business-registration renewal, registered office, company secretary, accounting, audit, and tax filing. Actual costs vary with transaction volume, payroll, turnover, industry, and whether the company is dormant.

Which Hong Kong business costs are easy to overlook?

Commonly overlooked costs include bookkeeping, annual audit, Profits Tax Return preparation, bank charges, licences, insurance, employment administration, and changes to corporate information. Companies may also pay extra for expedited filings, document certification, translations, or complex ownership and banking reviews. A realistic budget should separate mandatory government charges from professional and operating expenses.

Hidden Costs to Consider

Beyond the basic formation and compliance fees, keep these potential costs in mind:

  • Bank Account Opening — Some banks charge account opening fees (see our Bank Account Guide for details) (HK$0–1,000). Virtual banks are typically free.
  • Virtual Office — If you don't need physical space, virtual office services cost HK$100–500/month.
  • Corporate Seal — HK$200–500 for a company chop.
  • Translation Services — If documents need to be translated (e.g., for Mainland China use).
  • Registered Agent (offshore) — If you're a non-resident, you may need an offshore registered agent.

Ways to Reduce Costs

Here are some strategies to minimize your Hong Kong company formation expenses:

  • Use a formation agent package — Most agents bundle company secretary, registered address, and incorporation at a discounted rate.
  • Choose virtual office — Skip physical office space if you don't need client meetings.
  • Opt for online filing — Online incorporation is HK$5 cheaper and faster than paper filing.
  • Consolidate services — Use the same provider for company secretary, accounting, and audit to negotiate better rates.
  • Consider your fiscal year-end — Choosing March 31 as your year-end aligns with Hong Kong's tax year.

Pro Tip: If you're a foreign entrepreneur, factor in the cost of a Hong Kong bank account. Some banks require minimum deposits or charge monthly fees. Virtual banks like ZA Bank or WeLab are free to open and maintain.
