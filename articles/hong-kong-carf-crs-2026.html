Hong Kong Passes Landmark Crypto Tax Reporting Bill: What CARF and the Amended CRS Mean for Digital Asset Intermediaries

A practical guide for digital asset intermediaries preparing for the 2027 implementation of CARF and the amended Common Reporting Standard

What has Hong Kong just passed, and why does it matter?

On November 4, 2026, the Legislative Council passed the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amended Common Reporting Standard) Bill 2026, formalising Hong Kong's commitment to automatic crypto-tax information exchange starting in 2028 [GovHK, 4 Nov 2026] [IRD CARF Overview].

The bill transposes the OECD CARF (published 2023) and amended CRS into local law. This marks the end of tax anonymity for virtual assets in Hong Kong.

Hong Kong was identified by the OECD as immediately relevant to CARF and invited to implement by 2028 at the latest. The government has confirmed it will conduct the first automatic exchange starting in 2028 [1].

EXECUTIVE TAKEAWAY: The legislation gives RCASPs one year of due diligence and data collection (2027) before the first reporting cycle in 2028. However, immediate compliance resources must address the November 30 paper lodgement deadline under the M Code Block Extension Scheme.

Which entities must comply with CARF?

Any Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider (RCASP) with a reporting nexus to Hong Kong must comply. This includes licensed VATP exchanges, OTC desks, brokerages, and fund managers effectuating exchange transactions [IRD CARF Overview] [GovHK Consultation].

Reportable transactions cover three categories: crypto-to-fiat exchanges, crypto-to-crypto exchanges, and retail payment transactions. The data collected includes taxpayer identification numbers (TINs), residence jurisdiction, full legal name, date and place of birth, transaction values, and controlling persons of entity accounts [2].

The government has committed to conducting the first automatic exchange starting in 2028, on the condition that domestic legislation is in place by 2026 [1].

How does the amended CRS expand the reporting net?

The amended CRS closes the digital gap by expanding traditional financial account reporting to cover e-money products, central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), and specified digital financial products [Withers Worldwide] [GovHK Consultation].

Key changes include two new categories of digital money products:

  • Specified Electronic Money Products (SEMPs) — capture services like Octopus, AlipayHK, WeChat Pay HK, PayMe, and BoC Pay for the first time.
  • Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) — represent digital fiat currencies issued by central banks.

A de minimis exclusion applies to low-value SEMP accounts whose rolling average 90-day end-of-day account balance does not exceed HK$78,000 in any consecutive 90-day period [3].

The amended CRS also expands the definition of investment entities to include crypto-asset-related investment activities. Dual-reporting safeguards prevent duplicative reporting between CARF and the amended CRS for RFIs [4].

What are the penalties for non-compliance?

Section 80U of the Inland Revenue Ordinance establishes graduated sanctions, compounding mechanisms, and criminal exposure for willful failure to conduct due diligence or obtain self-certifications [Bill PDF Section 80U] [IRD CARF Overview].

The Bill also clarifies reasonable-excuse defences for corporate officers, modelled on Companies Ordinance protections. Proceedings for offences under sections 80R to 80W may be brought within six years after the offence was committed, even after entity dissolution [5].

STATUTORY WARNING: The six-year limitation period under Section 80U extends liability for specified officers who were in position immediately before dissolution. Compliance officers should document due diligence procedures carefully.

What about self-custodial wallets and the Travel Rule?

One of the key grey zones in CARF implementation is how self-custodial (unhosted) wallets are treated during retail transactions [Withers Worldwide] [OECD CARF Guide].

CARF does not require direct reporting of unhosted wallet transfers. However, transactions crossing an RCASP gateway trigger immediate reporting regardless of the target wallet architecture.

This means that even if a customer sends crypto from a self-custody wallet (e.g., MetaMask) to an exchange (RCASP), the exchange must capture and report the transaction. The SFC Travel Rule (AML/CFT transfer rules) operates alongside CARF reporting, requiring intermediaries to share originator and beneficiary information for transactions exceeding HK$15,000 [7].

Token classification remains another grey area. Utility tokens, fractionalised NFTs, and Specified Digital Financial Products (SDFPs) may attract different reporting treatments depending on their economic substance [3].

How does Hong Kong compare globally?

Hong Kong joins 27 jurisdictions undertaking their first CARF exchanges by 2028. This group includes Singapore, Switzerland, UAE, Thailand, and Australia [OECD CARF Commitments] [6].

The OECD identified Hong Kong as immediately relevant given its growing crypto-asset sector. Since 2018, Hong Kong has conducted automatic exchanges under the CRS with partner jurisdictions annually [8].

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Which entities are covered by CARF?

Any Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider (RCASP) with a reporting nexus to Hong Kong. This includes licensed VATP exchanges, OTC desks, brokerages, and fund managers. RCASPs must register with the IRD once the CARF Portal becomes available (expected Q4 2027) [1] [2].

Q2: When does CARF start in Hong Kong?

Reporting begins on January 1, 2027. RCASPs collect information from 2027 for the first automatic exchange in 2028. The OECD identified Hong Kong as immediately relevant [1] [6].

Q3: What transactions are reportable?

Three categories: crypto-to-fiat exchanges, crypto-to-crypto exchanges, and retail payment transactions. The law defines an exchange transaction broadly to cover both types [2].

Q4: How does the amended CRS differ?

It expands coverage to include digital money products (SEMPs and CBDCs), derivatives referencing crypto-assets, and investment entities engaged in crypto activities. Enhanced reporting includes gross proceeds from sale or redemption of financial assets [3] [4].

Q5: Are Octopus and AlipayHK now subject to CRS?

Yes. SEMP accounts include existing Stored Value Facility licensees such as Octopus, AlipayHK, WeChat Pay HK, PayMe, and BoC Pay. Accounts with a rolling average 90-day balance not exceeding HK$78,000 are exempt [3].

Q6: What happens on non-compliance?

Sections 80R through 80W establish graduated sanctions, compounding mechanisms, and criminal exposure. The Commissioner may compound offences. Proceedings for non-indictable offences may be brought within six years of the offence, even after entity dissolution [5].

Q7: Can corporate officers avoid personal liability?

Yes. The Bill clarifies reasonable-excuse defences for corporate officers, modelled on Companies Ordinance protections. Section 80U specifically addresses persons who were specified officers immediately before entity dissolution [5].

Q8: Is there duplication between CARF and amended CRS?

No. RFIs are not required to report gross proceeds under the amended CRS if already reported under CARF, though they may elect to report under both. This dual-reporting safeguard prevents double counting [4].

Q9: What data will be exchanged?

TINs, residence jurisdiction, full legal name, date and place of birth, transaction values, and controlling persons of entity accounts [2].

Q10: When is the amended CRS effective?

The amended CRS takes effect on January 1, 2028, with the first automatic exchange of data starting in 2029. RFIs must commence additional due diligence from January 2028 [1] [3].

Q11: What about immediate filing deadlines?

November 30, 2026 for paper lodgement and December 30, 2026 for electronic filing under the extended M Code Block Extension Scheme. This runs parallel to CARF preparation [7].

Q12: How do self-custodial wallets fit in?

CARF does not require direct reporting of unhosted wallet transfers. However, transactions crossing an RCASP gateway trigger immediate reporting regardless of target wallet architecture. The SFC Travel Rule (AML/CFT) runs alongside CARF for transactions above HK$15,000 [6] [3].
