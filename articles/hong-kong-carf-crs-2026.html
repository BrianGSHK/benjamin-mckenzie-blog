Crypto Tax & Reporting

If You Run a Crypto Business in Hong Kong, Here's What CARF Means for You

Hong Kong passed its landmark crypto tax reporting bill in November 2026. If you operate an exchange, OTC desk or fund, you've got one year to get reporting-ready.

You probably heard the headlines. On November 4, 2026, Hong Kong's Legislative Council passed the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (CARF and Amended CRS) Bill 2026. In plain terms, it brings crypto-assets under the same tax reporting net that's captured bank and investment accounts for years [GovHK] [IRD].

The bottom line: You get one year (2027) to collect data and set up systems before the first automatic exchange with the IRD in 2028. But there's a November 30 paper lodgement deadline if you need it for the M Code extension scheme — so don't wait until next year to start preparing.

Know who needs to report

If you're a crypto-asset service provider with a Hong Kong nexus, you're likely an RCASP — a Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider. That covers exchanges, OTC desks, brokerages and fund managers that handle exchange transactions. If in doubt, check whether you're effectuating transactions between crypto and fiat (or between two types of crypto) [IRD CARF Overview] [GovHK].

See what transactions you'll need to report

Three types of transactions are in scope:

  1. Crypto-to-fiat (e.g. Bitcoin to HKD)
  2. Crypto-to-crypto (e.g. USDC to ETH)
  3. Retail payment transactions in crypto

You'll report TINs, tax residency, full name, date and place of birth, transaction values and controlling persons for entity accounts [2].

Check how the amended CRS widens the net

The amended CRS adds two things that matter to crypto founders:

  • Digital money products — e-money like Octopus, AlipayHK, WeChat Pay HK, PayMe and BoC Pay (SEMP accounts). CBDCs are also in scope.
  • Crypto-related investments — the definition of investment entities now covers crypto-asset activities.

The de minimis rule helps: SEMP accounts with a rolling 90-day average balance under HK$78,000 are exempt [Withers] [GovHK].

Good news if you're an RFI (Registered Fund Intermediary): dual-reporting safeguards prevent you from reporting the same gross proceeds under both CARF and CRS [4].

Understand the penalties

Section 80U of the Inland Revenue Ordinance sets out graduated sanctions and compounding mechanisms. Willful failure to conduct due diligence or obtain self-certifications can trigger criminal exposure [Bill Section 80U] [IRD].

Watch out: You have six years from the offence to be prosecuted — and this period still applies even if the entity dissolves. If you were an officer right before dissolution, that personal liability clock is ticking. Document your due diligence now [5].

Self-custody wallets — do you report them?

Short answer: not directly. CARF doesn't require you to report transfers between unhosted (self-custody) wallets [Withers] [OECD].

But here's the catch — when a customer moves crypto from their own MetaMask wallet to your exchange, that transaction crosses an RCASP gateway and triggers reporting. The SFC Travel Rule (AML/CFT) also runs alongside CARF for transfers above HK$15,000 [7].

Token classification is still a bit unclear. Utility tokens, fractionalised NFTs and SDFPs may get treated differently depending on what they actually do in your business [3].

How Hong Kong stacks up globally

Hong Kong is one of 27 jurisdictions doing their first CARF exchange by 2028. You're joining Singapore, Switzerland, UAE, Thailand and Australia at the table [OECD CARF Commitments] [6].

The OECD flagged Hong Kong as immediately relevant given the sector's growth. Since 2018, HK has been doing CRS exchanges with partner jurisdictions every year [8].

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Which entities are covered by CARF?

Any crypto-asset service provider with a Hong Kong nexus — exchanges, OTC desks, brokerages and fund managers. You register with the IRD when the CARF Portal launches (expected Q4 2027) [1] [2].

Q2: When does CARF start?

Reporting starts January 1, 2027. You collect data through 2027 and file your first exchange by June 2028 [1] [6].

Q3: What transactions are reportable?

Crypto-to-fiat, crypto-to-crypto and retail payments. The law defines exchange broadly [2].

Q4: How does amended CRS differ?

It adds digital money products (SEMPs and CBDCs), crypto-linked derivatives and crypto investment activities. You also report gross proceeds from sales or redemptions [3] [4].

Q5: Are Octopus and AlipayHK in scope?

Yes — they're SEMP accounts. If your rolling 90-day average balance stays under HK$78,000, you're exempt [3].

Q6: What happens if I don't comply?

Graded fines, compounding penalties and possible criminal charges for willful failure. Six years from the offence to be prosecuted, even after entity dissolution [5].

Q7: Can officers avoid personal liability?

Yes — the Bill includes a reasonable-excuse defence for officers, modelled on the Companies Ordinance [5].

Q8: Do I report under both CARF and CRS?

No double reporting. If you report gross proceeds under CARF, you don't need to report them again under CRS — though you can if you want to [4].

Q9: What data do I collect?

TINs, tax residency, full name, date and place of birth, transaction values and controlling persons for entity accounts [2].

Q10: When does amended CRS kick in?

January 1, 2028 for new due diligence. First exchange under amended CRS in 2029 [1] [3].

Q11: What are the immediate deadlines?

November 30 for paper lodgement, December 30 for electronic filing under the M Code extension scheme [7].

Q12: Do I report unhosted wallet transfers?

Not directly. But when crypto crosses your RCASP gateway, it triggers reporting. The SFC Travel Rule also applies for transactions above HK$15,000 [6] [3].
