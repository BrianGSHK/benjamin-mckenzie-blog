Osome vs Sleek vs Statrys: Hong Kong Company Formation Compared (2026)

A transparent evidence review of three Hong Kong company-formation services, using linked customer-review excerpts, provider disclosures, official licensing references, and a repeatable comparison framework. Prices and review metrics are time-sensitive.

Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 11, 2026 · 10 min read

For context, read the TCSP licensing guide, the Hong Kong compliance checklist and our company-registration walkthrough before comparing providers.

Quick Answer: Choose Osome if you prioritise a digital-first workflow and a published lower starting price. Choose Sleek if you value a broader public review footprint and an all-in-one formation offer. Choose Statrys if multi-currency payments are central to your operating model. These are fit-based conclusions, not universal rankings.

How was this comparison prepared?

This review compares official provider pages, published package information and six identified Trustpilot review permalinks: two for each provider. Each sample contains one favourable and one unfavourable account. The review sample is transparent and traceable, but it is too small to represent all customers or calculate approval rates, average satisfaction or service quality.

Prices, promotions, review counts and package inclusions change. A headline price may exclude government charges, renewals, optional work or services performed by a separately regulated professional. The information below is a dated research snapshot checked on 21 August 2026. Ask each provider for a written scope and renewal schedule before paying.

How should founders compare Osome, Sleek and Statrys?

Compare three things in order: evidence traceability, scope clarity and operational fit. Evidence traceability means you can open the source behind a claim. Scope clarity means you understand first-year work, renewals, exclusions and professional responsibility. Operational fit means the provider suits your company’s banking, accounting, compliance, formation and communication needs.

A low advertised price is not automatically the lowest first-year cost. A high review count is not proof of consistent service. A payment account is not the same as a bank account, and no company-services provider can guarantee approval by a bank or payment institution.

What is the McKenzie fit framework?

The framework avoids an invented star ranking. Ask whether the provider gives you traceable evidence, a clear scope and a workable operating relationship. A founder who needs tax advice, audit sign-off or regulated payment services should identify the actual professional or institution responsible for that work, rather than assuming the platform handles everything itself.

What does evidence traceability mean?

Open the underlying pricing page, service page, licence record or review permalink. Record the date checked. For reviews, note that an individual account describes one customer’s experience, not the provider’s average outcome. For licensing, use the Companies Registry TCSP licence search where a TCSP check is relevant.

What does scope clarity mean?

Ask for one document showing the contracting entity, formation work, registered address, company-secretarial duties, accounting period, audit arrangement, tax-filing responsibility, government charges, renewal fees, optional services and support route. If the quotation does not separate one-off and recurring items, the comparison is incomplete.

What does operational fit mean?

Choose the workflow you can actually use. Some founders want a local relationship that coordinates several workstreams. Others prefer online onboarding and published plan architecture. A founder whose main need is multi-currency payment functionality should also test eligibility, transfer terms and the institution’s own due-diligence process.

What should you know about Osome?

Osome presents a digital-first Hong Kong company-formation and accounting workflow. Its published Hong Kong pages show Essential at HK$6,195, Comprehensive at HK$10,495 and Ecommerce Comprehensive at HK$13,083, alongside promotional figures displayed on the pricing page. Treat these as dated published prices, not a guaranteed final quotation.

Osome may fit a founder who wants a structured online process and can manage a checklist of add-ons and renewals. Before appointment, confirm the registered-address period, Annual Return coverage, accounting scope, tax filing, audit responsibility, document retention and escalation route. A digital interface may simplify onboarding, but it does not remove the need to understand who performs each professional service.

What did the two sampled Osome reviews indicate?

The selected sample contains one account describing helpful account-manager interaction and one account raising onboarding or contact concerns. These accounts are useful prompts for questions about response times, escalation and continuity. They do not establish a provider-wide service result, and they should not be converted into a satisfaction percentage.

What should you know about Sleek?

Sleek is positioned as a broader formation and corporate-services provider with a substantial public review presence. The selected review sample includes one favourable account describing professional or fast responses and one account raising a refund-delay concern. The sample is directional only and does not prove that the same experience will occur for another company.

Sleek may fit a founder who wants an all-in-one formation package and values a larger public review footprint as one research signal. Confirm the appointed service entity, TCSP position where relevant, registered-address scope, renewal price, ad-hoc change charges, accounting and audit boundaries, tax-filing responsibility and how refunds or disputes are escalated.

What should you verify about Sleek’s licence position?

Where a service involves company-secretarial or TCSP work, verify the relevant legal entity and licence status through the official Companies Registry search. A licence check supports regulatory due diligence; it does not guarantee response quality, pricing, continuity or a particular customer outcome.

What should you know about Statrys?

Statrys combines company-formation information with business payment and multi-currency support. That may be attractive to a founder whose daily operations involve receiving or sending several currencies. It also creates a sharper boundary to check: a payment account or payment service is not automatically a bank account, and eligibility remains subject to the institution’s own onboarding and risk decisions.

Statrys may fit a founder who has a genuine multi-currency operating need and wants to investigate company formation and payment functionality together. Ask whether the payment product is available to your company, what currencies and transfers are supported, what fees apply, how funds are safeguarded and which entity provides each service.

What did the two sampled Statrys reviews indicate?

The selected sample includes one account describing clear fees or responsive support and one account raising a transfer-service concern. These are individual observations, not an approval rate or a general finding about every payment or formation customer. Use them to test the provider’s support, complaints and transfer processes before signing.

Which provider fits different founder situations?

When might Osome be the better fit?

  • You prefer digital onboarding and a platform-led workflow.
  • You want to compare a published lower starting price.
  • You are comfortable checking registered-address, accounting, tax, audit and renewal add-ons.
  • You can obtain written confirmation of the support and escalation route.

When might Sleek be the better fit?

  • You value a broader formation package and a large public review footprint as one signal.
  • You want to compare company-service coverage beyond basic incorporation.
  • You are prepared to verify renewal pricing, change fees and the responsible professional.
  • You want to understand how refunds, handover and service disputes are handled.

When might Statrys be the better fit?

  • Multi-currency receipts, payments or transfers are central to the business.
  • You are willing to complete the institution’s own eligibility and due-diligence process.
  • You want to separate company formation from payment-account approval in your planning.
  • You will compare transfer fees, supported currencies, safeguarding and service boundaries.

When should you pause before choosing any of them?

Pause if your company has complex cross-border tax, regulated activities, unusual ownership, high-risk jurisdictions, material intellectual property or difficult banking facts. These providers may help with formation or administration, but they do not replace qualified legal, tax, audit or regulated financial advice where the risk is material.

What is the balanced verdict?

There is no universal winner. Osome is the clearest fit for a founder prioritising a digital-first workflow and published plan structure. Sleek is worth shortlisting where a broader formation package and public review footprint matter. Statrys is the most relevant option to investigate where multi-currency payments are a core operating requirement.

Before choosing, compare the written scope rather than the marketing headline. Confirm the legal entity, licence position where relevant, first-year and renewal costs, government charges, accounting and audit boundaries, tax responsibility, registered address, document handling and support escalation. Recheck prices and policies immediately before purchase.
