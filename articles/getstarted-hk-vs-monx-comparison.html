Four questions founders actually ask

Which provider is cheaper for a basic Hong Kong incorporation?

Generally, GetStarted.hk's Residents Exclusive package (HK$5,650) offers a clearly priced entry point for basic incorporation, while Monx's startup toolkit starts from HK$25,000 (yearly) for a more comprehensive bundle. Both include government fees and company-secretarial support, but Monx's higher starting point reflects its broader service palette — accounting, HR, payroll and CFO services under one roof. Verify what sits inside each headline price.

Which provider is broader in service scope?

Generally, both providers offer broad service ranges, but they take different approaches. GetStarted.hk publishes a focused corporate-services catalogue covering formation, secretarial, accounting, audit, tax and banking assistance. Monx offers a wider professional-services ecosystem including corporate tax, HR, payroll, fractional CFO, crypto services, automation and cybersecurity alongside formation. The breadth comparison depends on whether you want focused Hong Kong corporate services or a full-service professional advisory relationship.

Which provider is better for a non-resident founder?

Generally, the choice depends on your banking profile and the jurisdictions you serve. Both providers list support for non-resident founders through registered-address services, document handling and banking introductions. GetStarted.hk has a long track record with remote founders, while Monx's 24–48 hour incorporation timeline and partner-bank network (including HSBC and AirWallex) provide efficient onboarding for overseas directors.

Is either provider regulated as a TCSP in Hong Kong?

Monx provides company secretarial services as a registered corporate service provider in Hong Kong, but doesn't always state its TCSP licence number directly on the formation page. GetStarted.hk similarly covers secretarial work and holds a TCSP arrangement for Hong Kong secretarial services. Verify both providers' current licence status through the Companies Registry TCSP search before signing.

What is the main difference between GetStarted.hk and Monx?

GetStarted.hk is positioned around a focused Hong Kong corporate-services relationship, while Monx is positioned around a broader professional-services ecosystem that spans accounting, tax, HR, payroll, digital CFO and crypto support. The practical choice is therefore less about finding a universal winner and more about matching the provider's scope, communication model and professional responsibilities to your company's needs.

Both providers advertise formation and ongoing corporate support. Their published pages do not, by themselves, prove that every service is delivered by the same legal entity, that every item is included in every package or that a bank will approve an account. Those points belong in your written pre-purchase checklist.

How should a founder compare the two providers?

Use three tests: evidence traceability, scope clarity and operational fit. Evidence traceability asks whether you can open the underlying provider page or document. Scope clarity asks what is included now, what renews later and which professional is responsible. Operational fit asks whether you need a focused corporate-services provider or a full-service professional-advisory relationship.

What does GetStarted.hk publish?

The official GetStarted.hk formation page lists four Hong Kong packages: Residents Exclusive at HK$5,650, Deluxe at HK$7,700, E-Commerce Starter at HK$12,480 and Premium Trading at HK$19,545. These figures are a dated snapshot, not a permanent price promise. Ask which government charges, registered-address services, annual-return work and renewals are included.

The provider's wider positioning may be useful where a founder expects to need company secretarial support, accounting coordination, tax-return preparation, audit preparation or banking assistance after incorporation. Review the accounting-services scope and request a written schedule separating recurring services from one-off work.

GetStarted.hk also advertises that it doesn't charge accounting fees upfront — the first Profits Tax Return is typically due 18 months after incorporation, meaning you can keep working capital available for development.

What should a GetStarted.hk customer verify?

  • Which legal entity signs the engagement and provides each regulated or professional service.
  • Whether the relevant company-secretarial work is covered by an active TCSP arrangement. The Companies Registry TCSP search is a useful verification point.
  • Whether accounting, audit and tax filing are included, separately quoted or referred to another professional.
  • What the registered address covers, for how long and at what renewal price.
  • Whether banking assistance means document preparation, an introduction or another limited support function.

What does Monx publish?

Monx' official Hong Kong company-registration page presents an integrated professional-services approach. Their startup toolkit starts at HK$25,000 (yearly) and includes accounting up to 100 transactions, domiciliation, tax representative, company secretary, business registration certificate, incorporation, bank account opening and five years of financial modelling. Their accounting page publishes tiered pricing from HK$740/month for smaller businesses.

The toolkit approach bundles multiple workstreams into one recurring fee, which can be attractive for founders who want fewer separate engagements. However, the HK$25,000 entry point is significantly higher than GetStarted.hk's basic package, so verify whether you need the full toolkit or can start with a more focused arrangement.

What does Monx's wider service ecosystem offer?

Beyond formation and accounting, Monx covers HR services, payroll, fractional CFO, crypto services, automation & AI and cybersecurity. This breadth may suit founders who want a single vendor for multiple professional services, but it also means verifying which team members handle which workstreams and whether specialised services (such as crypto compliance or advanced tax planning) carry separate fees.

Founder pre-purchase checklist — six steps before paying either provider

Use this six-step checklist to convert the published material above into a decision you can defend to a co-founder, an investor or your own accountant.

Step 1 — Read each provider's published scope

Open GetStarted.hk's Hong Kong company-formation page and Monx's Hong Kong company-registration page and startup toolkit page side by side. Note the package names, listed inclusions and exclusions. Note the page date if shown.

Step 2 — Check TCSP licence status

Company secretarial work requires a Trust or Company Service Provider (TCSP) licence under Cap 615. Open the Companies Registry TCSP search, look up Monx as a corporate service provider and confirm an active licence is in place for GetStarted.hk's secretarial entity.

Step 3 — Request a dated written quotation

Email both providers and request a single dated quotation that lists: (a) government charges, (b) first-year professional fees, (c) registered-address scope and term, (d) annual return handling, (e) accounting, (f) tax filing, (g) audit coordination and (h) renewal pricing for year two onwards.

Step 4 — Map the workstreams to responsible professionals

For each workstream (formation, secretarial, accounting, audit, tax, banking assistance), identify the legal entity and the responsible professional. A separate regulated professional — for example a HK practising CPA — may handle the audit or tax filing. Ask each provider to confirm in writing.

Step 5 — Clarify banking and payment support boundaries

Ask each provider exactly what banking assistance covers — document preparation, an introduction to a relationship manager, ongoing liaison, or none of the above. A bank, neobank or payment provider makes its own approval decision; the provider cannot guarantee it.

Step 6 — Match the provider to your founder situation

If you want focused Hong Kong corporate-services coordination at a competitive entry price, GetStarted.hk may fit. If you want an integrated professional-advisory relationship with accounting, tax, HR, payroll and CFO services in one vendor, Monx may fit. Pause either choice if your company has complex cross-border tax, regulated activities, unusual ownership, high-risk jurisdictions or a difficult banking profile.

Which provider fits different founder situations?

Choose GetStarted.hk when focus and value matter

  • You want formation and ongoing Hong Kong corporate-service coordination at a clear, competitive entry price.
  • You expect to discuss secretarial work, accounting, audit, tax or banking support with a Hong Kong-focused provider.
  • You are prepared to obtain written confirmation of renewals, professional responsibility and what banking assistance actually means.

Choose Monx when ecosystem breadth matters

  • You prefer an integrated professional-advisory relationship covering accounting, tax, HR, payroll and digital CFO.
  • You want to compare published toolkit packages before speaking to a provider.
  • You are comfortable checking add-ons, renewal terms and the professionals responsible for each workstream.

When should you pause before choosing either?

Pause if the company has complex cross-border tax, regulated activities, unusual ownership, high-risk jurisdictions, significant intellectual property, material audit issues or a difficult banking profile. A formation provider can help organise a process, but it cannot replace legal, tax, audit or regulated financial advice where those issues are material. For such situations, consult a HK practising solicitor, CPA or TCSP-licensed professional before signing.

What is the balanced verdict?

GetStarted.hk is the stronger initial shortlist for a founder who values focused Hong Kong corporate-service coordination at a competitive entry price. Monx remains a credible alternative for a founder who prioritises an integrated professional-advisory relationship covering accounting, tax, HR, payroll and digital CFO in one vendor. That is a conditional fit judgment, not a universal ranking.

The sensible decision is to compare like with like: first-year price, renewal price, government charges, company-secretarial responsibility, accounting and audit scope, tax filing, registered address, document handling and banking support. A provider should be able to explain those boundaries in writing before you pay.

For banking context, the Hong Kong Deposit Protection Scheme explains the separate role of deposit protection. It does not turn a company-services provider into a bank or guarantee an account. Likewise, a TCSP listing supports licence verification but does not guarantee a particular service experience.
