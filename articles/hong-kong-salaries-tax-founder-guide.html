Hong Kong Salaries Tax for Founders: How to Report Income, Allowances and Deductions

A source-led planning guide for founders, directors and employees filing the BIR60, claiming allowances and choosing between progressive and standard-rate Salaries Tax.

Founder message

For a founder or employee, Salaries Tax is the personal income-tax side of doing business in Hong Kong. Whether the charge is triggered by a Hong Kong employment contract, a director's fee paid by your own HK company, or a pension sourced here, the Inland Revenue Department's Salaries Tax guide and the BIR60 return are the practical starting points. The current PAM 61(e) pamphlet confirms the 2026/27 allowances, the two-tier standard rates of 15% / 16%, and the maximum deduction limits you can claim. Read the official source, then talk to a HKICPA-registered adviser before filing.

Quick AnswerSalaries Tax is chargeable on income from any office, employment or pension sourced in Hong Kong. It is calculated at progressive rates on net chargeable income (income minus deductions minus allowances) or at the standard rate on net income (before allowances), whichever is lower. From 2024/25 a two-tier standard rate applies: 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 of net income and 16% on the remainder. Most individuals now do everything through the IRD eTAX Individual Tax Portal.

Who has to file a Hong Kong Salaries Tax return?

You generally need to file a Salaries Tax return (BIR60) if you have Hong Kong-sourced income from employment, a Hong Kong directorship or a pension. The IRD's Salaries Tax — What you need to know as an Employee page lists the chargeability triggers (office, employment, pension) and points you to the BIR60, allowances and deductions guidance. If you only have Profits Tax and no Hong Kong employment income, you may not need to file a BIR60 at all — but review the rules with an adviser before assuming so.

Per the IRD's Individuals landing page, separate personal-tax obligations exist for employees (including directors and pensioners), sole proprietors or partners, property owners, and non-resident individuals rendering services in Hong Kong. Each category has different forms, allowances and record-keeping rules, so confirm which one applies to your facts before completing the BIR60.

Is founder-director remuneration chargeable to Salaries Tax?

Yes. Salary, fees, bonuses, commissions and any director's remuneration paid for holding an office in a Hong Kong company are chargeable to Salaries Tax. Founder-directors therefore have a personal Salaries Tax side to every dividend-versus-salary decision, sitting alongside the company's Profits Tax position. Plan director pay and company profit extraction together, not separately.

Tax trap — director fees vs employee salary

Most generic tax guides lump "directors' fees" and "salaries" together. They are treated fundamentally differently under Hong Kong territorial-source rules, and the difference is one of the most common costly founder mistakes.

  • Director's fees from a Hong Kong-incorporated company are 100% chargeable to Salaries Tax under IRO section 9(1)(a)(ii)(A) — the "office" trigger. The fee is taxable whether the founder lives in Hong Kong, lives abroad, or visits Hong Kong for 30 days a year. The place-of-source rule follows the office (the company), not the physical location of the director.
  • Employee salary from a non-Hong Kong employment contract is charged only on the portion of services rendered in Hong Kong. Time-apportionment applies: services performed in Hong Kong are taxable, services performed elsewhere are not. The IRD's Salaries Tax guidance and case-law place-of-source tests (in particular IRC v Lin Meng Yuan and Hang Seng Bank v CIR) confirm that the source follows where the work is actually performed.

Why this matters. A founder-director of an HK company who lives abroad and visits Hong Kong twice a year cannot avoid Salaries Tax on the director's fee — the "office" is in Hong Kong regardless. But the same founder's salary from a separate non-HK employment is taxable only on the Hong Kong-days portion. Conflating the two is the single most frequent Salaries Tax mistake for cross-border founders.

The IRD's Salaries Tax guidance treats a director's fee as chargeable income even where the director physically sits outside Hong Kong, subject to specific cross-border facts. Combined with the company's Profits Tax rules (see First Profits Tax Return in Hong Kong), this is why many founder-directors benchmark both salaries tax and profits tax together when designing extraction. The offshore vs onshore comparison provides context for how a Hong Kong company can be Profits Tax–favourable while the director still owes personal Salaries Tax on director's fees.

How is Hong Kong Salaries Tax calculated?

Salaries Tax is calculated at progressive rates on net chargeable income (income minus deductions minus allowances) or at the standard rate on net income (before allowances), whichever is lower. From 2024/25 a two-tier standard rate applies: 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 of net income and 16% on the remainder. Tax payable is then reduced by any tax reduction announced in the Budget.

The current IRD PAM 61(e) — Allowances, Deductions and Tax Rate Table (August 2026) sets out the progressive-rate bands (2% / 6% / 10% / 14% / 17%) and the two-tier standard rates (15% / 16%) together with the most recent tax reduction schedule. The progressive rates have been stable for some years, while the standard rate moved from a flat 15% to the two-tier structure starting in 2024/25 — confirm which schedule applies to the year of assessment you are filing.

Worked example: founder-director on HK$600,000 salary

Worked example (anonymised)

Consider a single founder-director living in Hong Kong, drawing HK$600,000 in director's fees from their own HK private company in 2026/27. Apply the maximum MPF employee deduction (HK$18,000 statutory cap), the Basic Allowance (HK$145,000), and assume no other deductions or allowances. Income after MPF = HK$582,000; net chargeable income after the Basic Allowance = HK$437,000.

Progressive-rate outcome. Apply the 2026/27 progressive bands (2% / 6% / 10% / 14% / 17%) to HK$437,000. The cumulative tax on HK$437,000 at the published progressive bands is approximately HK$38,480.

Two-tier standard-rate outcome. 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 of net income = 15% × HK$582,000 = HK$87,300.

Result. Progressive-rate wins by a wide margin at this income level (HK$38,480 vs HK$87,300). This is the common pattern for mid-income Hong Kong residents; the two-tier standard rate only becomes favourable once allowances shrink to zero and net income climbs into the high six figures or above. Run both methods using the IRD's 2026/27 computation page for your specific numbers, and remember that any Budget-announced tax reduction is applied to whichever result is lower.

This anonymised example is illustrative only. The IRD assesses each taxpayer on the facts of their own year of assessment; cross-border situations, multiple employers, mid-year directorship changes, MPF offset rules and family-status elections all change the outcome. Confirm with a HKICPA-registered adviser before relying on a specific number.

What are the main Salaries Tax deductions for 2026/27?

Deductions reduce income chargeable to tax before allowances. The main items are MPF or Recognized Retirement Scheme contributions (HK$18,000 cap), home loan interest (HK$100,000 basic plus HK$20,000 additional from 2024/25), qualifying Voluntary Health Insurance Scheme (VHIS) premiums (HK$8,000 per insured person), qualifying annuity premiums and Tax Deductible MPF Voluntary Contributions (HK$60,000 combined), domestic rents (HK$100,000 basic plus HK$20,000 additional from 2024/25), self-education expenses (HK$100,000), elderly residential care expenses (HK$110,000 from 2026/27), approved charitable donations (35% of assessable income after allowable expenses and depreciation) and Assisted Reproductive service expenses (HK$100,000 cap from 2024/25).

Each deduction has its own qualifying conditions. For example, MPF contributions only count up to the statutory HK$18,000 cap regardless of how much the employer and employee actually paid, and home loan interest deduction is normally limited to one property and a fixed period. The full schedule and conditions are in the IRD's PAM 61(e) pamphlet and the Salaries Tax overview. Cross-reference the figures with the year of assessment you are filing, as the home-loan, domestic-rent and AR-expense deductions were all expanded from 2024/25.

Place of residence / housing benefit optimization strategy

Hong Kong allows an employer to provide housing to an employee or director. The IRD does not add the actual rent paid by the company to the employee's assessable income. Instead, it assesses a deemed "Rental Value" benefit using a statutory formula that almost always comes in well below the cash rent — making employer-provided housing one of the most tax-efficient compensation levers available to founder-directors.

The IRD's formula (per IRO section 9(1)(a) and the department's published guidance) treats the benefit as 10% of the employee's net income from employment (after deductions but before allowances), less any rent the employee pays back to the employer for the same property. The actual cash rent paid by the company to the landlord is not added to assessable income.

Worked example (anonymised) — HK$1M salary + HK$500k direct rent reimbursement

Consider a founder-director whose HK private company pays HK$1,000,000 in director's fees and also pays HK$500,000 of rent directly to the landlord on the founder's behalf, with no rent recovered from the founder.

  • Director's fees (cash remuneration) = HK$1,000,000
  • Rental Value benefit (added by IRD) = 10% × HK$1,000,000 = HK$100,000
  • Cash rent paid by company to landlord = HK$500,000 (not added to assessable income)
  • Total assessable income for Salaries Tax = HK$1,000,000 + HK$100,000 = HK$1,100,000

Result. The founder is taxed on HK$1,100,000 even though they received HK$1,500,000 of total economic value (cash + housing). Effectively HK$400,000 of rent is tax-free at the personal level. The same structure would also let the company deduct the HK$500,000 rent as an operating expense against assessable Profits Tax, subject to the usual "wholly and exclusively" test and IRD transfer-pricing rules on related-party transactions.

If the founder pays any rent back to the company (for example, HK$120,000 per year), the deemed Rental Value benefit is reduced by the recovered amount — so the IRD would add HK$100,000 − HK$120,000 = HK$0 (a negative figure is treated as zero). Some founders deliberately set the recovered rent just above 10% of net income to wipe out the deemed benefit entirely. Confirm the structure with a HKICPA-registered adviser and document the arrangement in the company's board minutes before relying on it.

Should I use the progressive rate or the two-tier standard rate?

Use whichever gives the lower figure. The IRD assesses both methods automatically, so the taxpayer usually does not need to elect between them. As a practical check: progressive rates often win at lower incomes and where allowances are significant; the two-tier standard rate (15% / 16%) often wins for high earners without large allowances, especially once net income passes HK$5,000,000. Run both using the IRD's 2026/27 Salaries Tax computation page to confirm.

The IRD's per-year-of-assessment tax computation pages are the simplest way to verify the rate that applies to your case. The page for 2026/27 contains the official computation form. The pages for 2025/26 and 2024/25 are still useful for older filings.

How do I file and pay Salaries Tax in Hong Kong?

Most individual taxpayers now file Salaries Tax returns online through the IRD's eTAX Individual Tax Portal (ITP), with electronic storage of supporting documents and electronic tax payment. Paper BIR60 returns are still available where the IRD issues them, but electronic filing is the default for new and existing individual filers. Confirm your filing channel from the BIR60 cover, lodge before the issue-date deadline and keep digital copies of all supporting evidence.

Worked example 2: founder-director on HK$6,000,000 salary

Worked example (anonymised, high-earner)

Now consider a single founder-director of a Hong Kong private company drawing HK$6,000,000 in director's fees in 2026/27, with the maximum MPF employee deduction (HK$18,000), no TVC or annuity premiums, and the Basic Allowance (HK$145,000). Net income after MPF = HK$5,982,000; net chargeable income after the Basic Allowance = HK$5,837,000.

Progressive-rate outcome. Apply the 2026/27 progressive bands (2% / 6% / 10% / 14% / 17%) to HK$5,837,000. Cumulative tax at the published progressive bands is approximately HK$925,150 (the top band is reached at HK$100,001 and above, so almost all the income is taxed at 17%).

Two-tier standard-rate outcome. 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 of net income (HK$750,000) plus 16% on the remainder (HK$982,000 × 16% = HK$157,120) gives total HK$907,120.

Result. The two-tier standard rate wins by approximately HK$18,000 even after the Basic Allowance — a much narrower margin than the low-income case. Once deductions shrink to the statutory floor and the Basic Allowance is the only allowance available, the two-tier standard rate becomes the default for founders with HK$5M-plus net income. Run both methods using the IRD's 2026/27 computation page for your specific numbers; family-status elections (joint assessment with a non-earning spouse) can flip the result back to progressive.

Decision tree: should I take salary or dividends?

Decision tree

For founder-directors of Hong Kong private companies, the practical choice is how much to extract as Salaries Tax–chargeable director's fees versus franked dividends (which are not charged to Salaries Tax in Hong Kong and not subject to withholding tax on dividends paid from HK companies). A simple rule of thumb:

  • Net income below HK$1.5M after MPF and allowances? Progressive rates almost always win. Take salary up to the Basic Allowance, then balance dividends for the rest.
  • Net income HK$1.5M – HK$5M? Run progressive-rate and two-tier-standard-rate calculations each year; the answer swings on family-status elections and the size of allowances. Model with both methods.
  • Net income above HK$5M? Two-tier standard rate usually wins. Cap salary at the level where progressive no longer helps, then take the balance as dividends (subject to Profits Tax treatment on the company side).
  • Cross-border situation? Place-of-source rules apply. Income sourced outside Hong Kong may escape Salaries Tax altogether — review the case-law test (where the work is actually performed) with a HKICPA-registered adviser before deciding how much to extract.

This is a starting heuristic, not tax advice. The IRD assesses each founder-director on the actual facts of the year of assessment, and the company side must also hold up — Profits Tax rules apply to the salary deduction at the company level. See our First Profits Tax Return in Hong Kong guide for the company-side framework.

The salary vs dividend crossover point

The right answer depends on three rates: (a) the company's Profits Tax rate on the marginal dollar, (b) the founder's marginal Salaries Tax rate, and (c) the proportion of profit actually paid out. The IRD's current Profits Tax rates are 8.25% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profit and 16.5% on the remainder; Salaries Tax reaches a top marginal rate of 17% under the progressive bands or 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 and 16% above under the two-tier standard rate.

Once the founder reaches the two-tier standard rate band (15% / 16%) and the company is above the HK$2M two-tier band (16.5%), paying more salary rarely beats paying a dividend — the tax savings at the company side are roughly cancelled by the Salaries Tax cost at the founder side. The crossover is therefore usually somewhere between the founder's basic-allowance-adjusted net income and the two-tier standard-rate threshold, not at any single number.

How does a founder choose between Salaries Tax extraction and dividends?

For a founder-director of a Hong Kong private company, the practical decision is how much to extract as director's fees (Salaries Tax) versus dividends (which are not subject to Hong Kong withholding tax and not currently charged to Salaries Tax). Both sides of the comparison must hold up: the company must be able to justify the salary as an allowable deduction for Profits Tax, and the founder must accept that director's fees are personal Salaries Tax income. Modelling both tax outcomes together avoids the common mistake of optimising Profits Tax in isolation.

Taxation of founder share options, RSUs and vesting

Most early-stage founder-director compensation is partly equity — share options, restricted stock units (RSUs) or direct share grants. Hong Kong taxes these gains under IRO section 9(1)(d) and the IRD's DIPN 36 practice note, with rules that differ materially from the cash-salary treatment above.

When the tax is triggered

  • Share options granted by reason of employment are taxed at the moment of exercise, not at grant and not at vesting. The IRD's default position is that nothing happens at grant or at vesting for tax purposes.
  • RSUs (which typically settle in shares on vesting without an exercise step) are taxed at the moment the shares vest or are delivered to the employee, whichever the plan documents define as the "chargeable event".
  • Direct share grants are taxed at the moment the shares are delivered and become the employee's property.

How the taxable gain is calculated

The taxable amount is the difference between the market value of the shares at the chargeable event and the price the employee paid (if any). For a private or unlisted company — the common case for founders and early hires — DIPN 36 sets out how the IRD will approach valuation. The IRD will generally accept:

  • an independent valuation report prepared at the relevant date;
  • the most recent arm's-length price from a third-party investment round (a "preferred series" financing), adjusted for any discount where the new investor received rights not available to existing optionholders; or
  • the intrinsic value of the option at exercise (market value less strike price), where the option is in the money and there is no reason to discount.

The IRD will scrutinise closely discounted or back-dated valuations. Founders who grant themselves options at a strike above market value or who exercise immediately after a low-value round may face additional tax, penalties and interest under the IRO.

What happens if a founder leaves Hong Kong before exercising

For share options granted before the founder ceased HK employment, the IRD's long-standing practice (reflected in DIPN 36 and various Board-departmental Determination cases) is that the gain is still taxable in Hong Kong if the option is exercised while the founder remains tax-resident, and is also potentially taxable if exercised within a "reasonable time" after departure (the IRD sometimes treats a one-to-two-year window as reasonable, sometimes much shorter). Founders who plan to leave Hong Kong before exercising should consider:

  • timing the exercise to coincide with a low-valuation round rather than a high-valuation round;
  • documenting the IRD practice in the option-departure documents themselves (the option plan, board minutes and grant letters); and
  • seeking a tax-clearance letter (IRD's "letter of no objection" or specific ruling) before departure if the gain is material.

The BIR60 treats share-option gains as income from employment, not as capital gains. There is no separate capital-gains regime in Hong Kong, but the IRD's classification of an option gain as employment income means it lands in the same Salaries Tax return as the founder's salary and other employment income. Founders who are dual-tax-resident in another jurisdiction may also face tax there on the same gain — double-tax relief depends on the relevant DTA.

Frequently asked questions

Who has to file a Hong Kong Salaries Tax return?

Anyone who earns income from Hong Kong employment, a Hong Kong pension or an office/directorship held in Hong Kong generally has a charge to Salaries Tax. The IRD's Salaries Tax guide explains who is chargeable, the role of the BIR60 return and the obligations of employees, directors and pensioners.

Is director's remuneration from my Hong Kong company assessable to Salaries Tax?

Yes. Salary, fees, bonuses, commissions and any director's remuneration paid for holding an office in a Hong Kong company are chargeable to Salaries Tax, regardless of where the director physically sits, subject to specific cross-border facts. Founder-directors should therefore plan their extraction from the company with both Profits Tax and Salaries Tax in view.

How is Hong Kong Salaries Tax calculated?

Salaries Tax is calculated at progressive rates on net chargeable income (income minus deductions minus allowances) or at the standard rate on net income (before allowances), whichever is lower. From 2024/25 a two-tier standard rate applies: 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 of net income and 16% on the remainder.

What are the main Salaries Tax allowances and deductions for 2026/27?

The IRD's PAM 61(e) pamphlet sets out the current allowances and deductions, including the Basic Allowance (HK$145,000 from 2026/27), Married Person's Allowance, Child Allowance, Single Parent Allowance, Personal Disability Allowance and the maximum deduction limits for MPF, home loan interest, domestic rents, qualifying annuity premiums and MPF voluntary contributions, qualifying VHIS premiums, self-education expenses, charitable donations and AR service expenses.

Can I file and pay Hong Kong Salaries Tax online?

Yes. The IRD operates the eTAX Individual Tax Portal (ITP), which supports online filing of the BIR60, electronic storage of supporting documents and electronic tax payment. Most taxpayers now file Salaries Tax returns electronically rather than on paper.

How does Hong Kong decide if my employment income is Hong Kong-sourced?

The IRD applies a place-of-source rule grounded in case law (in particular the IRC v Lin Meng Yuan and Hang Seng Bank v CIR line of cases). Income from employment is generally sourced where the employment is exercised — typically where the employee physically performs the work — not where the contract is signed. A founder-director who works entirely outside Hong Kong may escape Salaries Tax on that income, but cross-border facts (trips to HK, services rendered here, deputisation of authority) can change the answer.

What is the two-tier standard rate and when does it apply?

From the year of assessment 2024/25 onwards, the standard rate is split into two tiers: 15% on the first HK$5,000,000 of net income and 16% on the portion above HK$5,000,000. The IRD assesses both the progressive-rate and two-tier-standard-rate methods automatically and charges the lower of the two.

What is a Tax Reserve Certificate (TRC) and when is it useful?

A Tax Reserve Certificate is an IRD-issued instrument you can buy to pre-fund a likely Salaries Tax liability and earn interest at the prevailing rate. It is typically used by taxpayers with non-PAYE income (rental income, director's fees, bonuses paid outside Hong Kong) where regular payroll withholding is not available. The certificate is then applied against the final tax demand.

Do I need to file a Salaries Tax return if I have only Profits Tax and no Hong Kong employment income?

Not usually. If you have no Hong Kong-sourced employment income, no director's fee from a Hong Kong company and no Hong Kong pension, the BIR60 is generally not required. You still need to file Profits Tax if the company derives Hong Kong-sourced assessable profits. Confirm with a HKICPA-registered adviser if your facts are mixed.

What is provisional Salaries Tax and how is it different from the final bill?

Provisional Salaries Tax is an in-year payment the IRD demands roughly three months before the year of assessment ends, based on your prior-year charge. The final Salaries Tax bill is assessed after the BIR60 is lodged and reconciled with the provisional payment. Any over-provision is refunded; any under-provision is billed.

How does MPF interact with Salaries Tax?

Mandatory and voluntary MPF contributions by an employee count as a deduction against Salaries Tax, capped at HK$18,000 in aggregate for the statutory cap. Tax Deductible MPF Voluntary Contributions (TVC) share a separate combined cap of HK$60,000 with qualifying annuity premiums (TVC + Qualifying Annuity Premium = HK$60,000). Employer contributions are not taxable in the employee's hands but are not deductible either.

What happens if I change employers mid-year or take up a new directorship?

Each new employer or company must be reported on the BIR60, including the portion of income earned. The previous employer's IR56B and the new employer's IR56E together cover the change. Director's fees are usually paid annually or in arrears, so report the full year of assessment on a single BIR60.

Is severance pay, terminal payment or long-service payment taxable?

Statutory severance pay and long-service payment under the Employment Ordinance are partly exempt from Salaries Tax — up to 50% of the employee's final total emoluments (or the portion calculated under the Ordinance, whichever is lower). Anything above that ceiling is taxable. Contractual gratuities outside the Ordinance are fully taxable. Confirm treatment with the IRD's guidance or a HKICPA-registered adviser.

What are the penalties for late filing or incorrect Salaries Tax returns?

Failure to file the BIR60 on time, or making an incorrect return without reasonable excuse, can trigger IRD penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. The IRD may impose additional tax (up to three times the undercharged amount) and the taxpayer remains liable for the original tax plus interest. The IRD also publishes settlement practices for compounding offences; voluntary disclosure before an IRD enquiry is generally treated more favourably than detection-led assessment.

Is a director's fee from my Hong Kong company taxable even if I live overseas?

Yes. A director's fee from a Hong Kong-incorporated company is fully chargeable to Hong Kong Salaries Tax under IRO section 9(1)(a)(ii)(A), regardless of where the director physically lives or how many days they spend in Hong Kong. The fee follows the office (the HK company), not the director's location. This is the most common costly founder mistake.

How does the IRD treat employer-provided housing for a founder-director?

The IRD adds a deemed 'Rental Value' benefit equal to 10% of net income from employment (after deductions but before allowances), less any rent the employee pays back to the employer. The actual cash rent paid by the company is not added to assessable income. For a HK$1M salary and HK$500k of company-paid rent, the founder is taxed on HK$1.1M, leaving HK$400k of rent effectively tax-free.

Is employer-provided housing tax-free for employees in Hong Kong?

Not entirely. The IRD deems a Rental Value benefit equal to 10% of net employment income, less any rent recovered from the employee. The company-paid cash rent itself is not added to assessable income, so the structure is still highly tax-efficient compared to a cash salary of equivalent value.

Should I take salary or dividends from my Hong Kong company?

The crossover depends on your marginal Salaries Tax rate versus the company's marginal Profits Tax rate. Salaries Tax tops at 17% progressive (or 15%/16% two-tier); Profits Tax is 8.25% on the first HK$2M of assessable profit and 16.5% thereafter. Salary saves net tax when your Salaries Tax marginal is lower than the company's Profits Tax marginal on the corresponding dollar.

At what income level does the two-tier standard rate beat the progressive rate?

For a single taxpayer with the Basic Allowance only, the progressive rate almost always wins because HK$145,000 is removed from the tax base before any rate applies. The two-tier standard rate only catches up for non-residents who forgo allowances, or where family-status elections change the picture. The crossover band is roughly HK$1.8M–HK$2.4M of net income for a single founder.

How are share options taxed in Hong Kong?

Under IRO section 9(1)(d) and DIPN 36, share options granted by reason of employment are taxed at the moment of exercise — not at grant or vesting. The taxable gain is the market value of the shares at exercise minus the option price paid. For unlisted companies the IRD accepts independent valuations, recent third-round investment prices, or intrinsic value at exercise.

Are RSUs taxed the same as share options in Hong Kong?

RSUs are taxed at the moment the shares vest or are delivered (the 'chargeable event'), at market value at that point. There is no option strike to subtract, so the entire vesting value is assessable income from employment and lands in the BIR60 alongside salary and director's fees.

What happens to share options if I leave Hong Kong before exercising?

If you exercise while still HK tax-resident the gain is taxable in Hong Kong. If you exercise after leaving Hong Kong, the IRD's practice (per DIPN 36) is generally that the gain is still HK-taxable if exercised within a 'reasonable time' of departure. Founders planning to leave should seek a tax-clearance letter or specific ruling before departure if the gain is material.

How do I file Hong Kong Salaries Tax online?

Most taxpayers file via the IRD eTAX Individual Tax Portal (ITP): log in with HKID, complete the BIR60, attach supporting documents, and submit electronically. The portal allows electronic tax payment and stores BIR60 records for at least seven years. Use the BIR60 pre-filing worksheet to capture all deductible items (MPF, housing benefit, share-option gains, severance) before you start.

What is the BIR60 and when is it due?

The BIR60 is the Hong Kong Salaries Tax return issued by the IRD. The IRD typically issues BIR60s in early May, with a one-month filing deadline (often extended). Salaried employees with no HK income other than the employer's payroll data usually do not receive a BIR60 and are taxed under the 'salary tax assessment' process instead.

Is a non-resident founder still liable for Hong Kong Salaries Tax?

A non-resident is liable only on income sourced in Hong Kong. Director's fees from a HK company are HK-sourced by virtue of the office (so fully taxable), but employment income from a non-HK contract is taxable only on the Hong Kong-days portion. Non-residents do not receive the Basic Allowance or Married Person's Allowance unless they elect to be treated as ordinarily resident.

3. BIR60 founder pre-filing worksheet

Before logging into the IRD eTAX Individual Tax Portal to file the BIR60, capture the following figures in a single worksheet. This catches the most common omissions (MPF over-deduction, missing place-of-source apportionment, housing benefit) before they become IRD enquiries.

  • Identity and employment: HKID, year of assessment, ordinary resident election (yes/no), number of employers / directorships held during the year.
  • Income lines: salary, director's fees, bonus, commission, share-option gain at exercise (MV − option price), RSU vesting value, housing benefit (Rental Value = 10% × net income − rent recovered), other taxable emoluments.
  • Deductions (each with statutory cap): MPF employee contribution (cap HK$18,000), MPF TVC + QAP combined (cap HK$60,000), home loan interest (cap HK$100,000 + HK$20,000 additional), domestic rents (cap HK$100,000 + HK$20,000), VHIS premiums (HK$8,000 per insured person), self-education (HK$100,000), charitable donations (35% of assessable income), elderly residential care (HK$110,000 from 2026/27), AR service expenses (HK$100,000).
  • Allowances (each taxpayer's facts): Basic Allowance HK$145,000 / Married Person's Allowance HK$290,000 / Child Allowance HK$140,000 each (+HK$140,000 newborn bump) / Single Parent Allowance HK$145,000 / Dependent Parent and Grandparent Allowance / Personal Disability Allowance / Disabled Dependant Allowance.
  • Place-of-source facts: total days worked in Hong Kong during the year of assessment (cross-border employees only) and the basis for any apportionment.
  • Tax Reserve Certificate purchases during the year (income for the BIR60, deduction for the TRC).

Pre-filling the BIR60 with this worksheet cuts the eTAX input time, reduces error rates and produces a paper trail if the IRD asks follow-up questions later.
