Four questions founders actually ask

Which provider is cheapest for a basic Hong Kong incorporation?

CheapInCorp's published price is the lowest of the three at HK$5,400. GetStarted.hk's entry-level Residents Exclusive package is HK$5,650 and its Deluxe pack — described on the formation page as "Most Popular" — is HK$7,700. Monx does not publish a price; its contact promise is "Cost effective quote". A lower headline price may exclude government charges, registered-address renewal or annual-return work; ask each provider for a dated written quotation.

Which provider has the broadest service catalogue in Hong Kong?

GetStarted.hk, based on its published formation page and wider service menu. Its service catalogue covers formation, company secretarial, registered address, accounting, audit coordination, tax filing, banking-related assistance and (in the Premium Trading pack) HSBC HK / DBS / ZA Bank introductions. Monx's Hong Kong service catalogue covers Company Registration, Company Secretary, Accounting, Corporate Tax Services, HR Services and Payroll, Digital CFO, Crypto Services, Cybersecurity and Automation & AI, but at a quote-led price rather than a published menu. CheapInCorp's catalogue covers incorporation, registered address, company secretary, annual return, accounting & bookkeeping, audit & tax filing and bank account opening as separate pages on its services menu.

Which provider is the strongest fit for a non-resident founder?

Depends on your banking profile and the jurisdictions you serve, not the provider label. A non-resident founder typically needs a registered-address service, certified document handling and a higher-touch banking introduction. GetStarted.hk's Premium Trading pack is the only one of the three that names specific bank introductions (HSBC HK, DBS, ZA Bank). Monx's Crypto Services and Digital CFO workstreams suggest a stronger fit for non-resident or token-related businesses, subject to a dated quotation. CheapInCorp's Why Us Over Others page claims instant human response and a 95%+ bank account opening success rate; that claim is provider-published and not independently audited.

Is any of the seven a TCSP (Trust or Company Service Provider) licensee?

No public statement on any of the seven service pages directly confirms a Trust or Company Service Provider (TCSP) licence number. TCSP licensing is required under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615) for company secretarial work in Hong Kong. Verify each provider's legal entity on the Companies Registry TCSP search before signing an engagement letter. See also the dedicated TCSP licensing guide on benjaminmckenzie.com.

How should a founder compare the three providers?

Use three tests: evidence traceability, scope clarity and operational fit. Evidence traceability asks whether you can open the underlying provider page or document. Scope clarity asks what is included now, what renews later and which professional is responsible. Operational fit asks whether you need a relationship-led service, a multi-jurisdiction relationship or the lowest flat fee.

Founder pre-purchase checklist — six steps before paying any of the three

Use this six-step checklist to convert the published material above into a decision you can defend to a co-founder, an investor or your own accountant.

Step 1 — Read each provider's published scope

Open GetStarted.hk's Hong Kong company-formation page, Monx's Company Registration page and CheapInCorp's Incorporate A Company page side by side. Note the package names, listed inclusions and exclusions, and whether a price is published. Note the page date if shown.

Step 2 — Check TCSP licence status for each legal entity

Company secretarial work requires a Trust or Company Service Provider (TCSP) licence under Cap 615. Open the Companies Registry TCSP search, look up the legal entity behind each provider and confirm an active licence is in place. For GetStarted.hk, look up "Get Started HK Limited". For Monx and CheapInCorp, identify the contracting entity from the engagement letter and look up that entity.

Step 3 — Request a dated written quotation from all three

Email GetStarted.hk and CheapInCorp and submit the Monx contact form. Request a single dated quotation from each that lists: (a) government charges, (b) first-year professional fees, (c) registered-address scope and term, (d) annual return handling, (e) accounting, (f) tax filing, (g) audit coordination and (h) renewal pricing for year two onwards. For Monx, also request the engagement-letter counterparty and a separate fee for each additional jurisdiction.

Step 4 — Map the workstreams to responsible professionals

For each workstream (formation, secretarial, accounting, audit, tax, banking assistance), identify the legal entity and the responsible professional. A separate regulated professional — for example a HK practising CPA — may handle the audit or tax filing. Ask each provider to confirm in writing.

Step 5 — Clarify banking and payment support boundaries

Ask each provider exactly what banking assistance covers — document preparation, an introduction to a relationship manager, ongoing liaison, or none of the above. GetStarted.hk's Premium Trading pack lists HSBC HK / DBS / ZA Bank introductions. CheapInCorp's Why Us Over Others page claims a 95%+ bank account opening success rate. Monx lists Banking and e-Wallet as a Corporate Maintenance service. A bank, neobank or payment provider makes its own approval decision; the provider cannot guarantee it.

Step 6 — Match the provider to your founder situation

If you want a published, dated Hong Kong package menu and a local relationship, GetStarted.hk may fit. If you want a quote-led multi-jurisdiction model with Digital CFO and Crypto Services, Monx may fit. If you want the lowest flat fee for a no-frills Hong Kong incorporation, CheapInCorp may fit. Pause any choice if your company has complex cross-border tax, regulated activities, unusual ownership, high-risk jurisdictions or a difficult banking profile.

A TCSP is a Trust or Company Service Provider licensed under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615) by the Hong Kong Companies Registry. How to use this comparison

Provider decision framework

Choose on the work your company needs after incorporation, not on the headline first-year price alone. A lower entry price may be reasonable for a founder who already has an accountant and registered-address solution; a bundled package may be better when the founder needs company secretarial work, accounting, tax filing and banking preparation in one workflow.

  • Scope: separate incorporation, secretary, registered address, accounting, audit and tax filing.
  • Evidence: ask for the current package page, exclusions and renewal price in writing.
  • Fit: match the provider to transaction volume, non-resident complexity and the founder's need for human review.

Comparison checklist

  1. Save the date and URL of every price checked.
  2. Separate government fees from provider fees.
  3. Record what is excluded, including audit, tax filing and banking support.
  4. Check renewal pricing and cancellation terms.
  5. Verify any licensing or regulated-service claim against the relevant official register.
