GetStarted.hk vs Osome: Hong Kong Company Services Compared (2026)

A source-led comparison of two Hong Kong company-service providers, covering formation, company secretarial work, accounting, tax, banking support, pricing scope and founder fit.

Benjamin McKenzie
Aug 21, 2026 · 10 min read

For background, see our Hong Kong company registration guide, annual compliance guide, startup-cost guide, business banking guide and TCSP licensing guide.

Quick Answer: GetStarted.hk may be the stronger fit for founders who want a Hong Kong-focused relationship spanning formation, company secretarial work, accounting, audit, tax coordination, registered-office support and banking assistance. Osome may suit founders who prefer a digital-first platform with published Hong Kong incorporation and accounting offers. The choice should depend on scope, recurring cost, entity responsibility, licensing and the level of human support you need.

Methodology: how this comparison was prepared

I compared the providers’ current official Hong Kong pages and Osome’s official investor page, checked on 21 August 2026. Prices, review counts and package inclusions are provider-published claims, not an independent audit of service quality. I have not treated a bank or payment-account introduction as a guarantee of approval.

The comparison is deliberately conditional. A headline price may exclude recurring work, optional services, government charges or work performed by a separately regulated professional. Ask each provider for a written first-year and renewal-cost schedule.

How do GetStarted.hk and Osome differ?

Both providers present online routes for company formation and ongoing administration. The practical difference is emphasis: GetStarted.hk presents a broader Hong Kong corporate-services model, while Osome presents a more productised digital platform connecting incorporation, accounting, compliance and onboarding flows.

That distinction matters only if it matches your operating needs. A founder who wants one local team to coordinate accounting, audit and tax may value breadth. A founder who wants a structured online journey and clearly displayed plan architecture may value Osome’s platform approach.

Formation packages and price scope

GetStarted.hk: Its official formation page lists Residents Exclusive at HK$5,650, Deluxe at HK$7,700, E-Commerce Starter at HK$12,480 and Premium Trading at HK$19,545. The page says the latter three include the government fee. Confirm what renews after year one and whether accounting, audit and tax work is quoted separately.

Osome: Its official Hong Kong price page displays Essential at HK$6,195, Comprehensive at HK$10,495 and Ecommerce Comprehensive at HK$13,083, alongside lower figures shown on the page. Because the page displays multiple price states and promotions, request a dated written quotation identifying the applicable price, government fee, registered address, annual return and renewal scope.

Accounting, audit and tax support

GetStarted.hk’s accounting-services page describes bookkeeping, bank reconciliation, year-end accounts, audit preparation and Profits Tax Return support. Osome’s Hong Kong site promotes accounting and audit as a separate service line. In both cases, confirm which entity performs the statutory audit, who signs the audit, what records are included, and whether tax filing is an add-on.

Neither provider’s marketing page should replace advice from the company’s appointed auditor, tax representative or other qualified professional. Hong Kong companies should also distinguish simplified reporting eligibility from exemption from the statutory audit.

Banking and payment-account support

Both providers describe assistance with bank or payment-account onboarding. This is support, not approval. A bank, neobank or payment provider makes its own customer-due-diligence decision. Ask whether the service is an introduction, document review, application coordination or an account product; also ask whether deposits are protected under Hong Kong’s Deposit Protection Scheme.

Customer evidence and investor context

GetStarted.hk publishes provider-linked review and customer evidence on its own pages. Osome publishes customer evidence on its Hong Kong pages and identifies venture-capital partners on its official investor page. These are different types of evidence: reviews indicate reported customer experiences, while investor backing indicates financing and governance context. Neither proves that a service is suitable for every founder.

Osome’s investor page names Illuminate Financial, Constructor Capital, Target Global, AltaIR Capital, AFG Partners and S16 VC among its venture-capital partners. This helps explain Osome’s technology and scaling orientation; it is not a quality score.

Verdict: which provider should a founder shortlist?

GetStarted.hk — neutral to positive: On the published scope reviewed, GetStarted.hk deserves consideration where the founder values a wider Hong Kong service relationship rather than incorporation alone. Its apparent breadth is useful, but the positive conclusion is conditional: obtain written confirmation of recurring fees, regulated responsibilities, audit/tax scope, renewal terms and banking support before buying.

Osome — neutral to negative: Osome is a credible digital-first option with published Hong Kong plans and identifiable venture-capital backing. However, for a founder seeking hands-on, locally coordinated support across formation, accounting, audit, tax and ongoing company administration, the platform-led presentation may leave more scope and add-on questions to resolve. I would not choose it without a written service map and total-cost schedule.

Questions to ask before appointing either provider

  • Which legal entity will provide each service?
  • Is the company-secretarial provider licensed for the relevant TCSP activity?
  • What is included in year one, and what renews annually?
  • Are accounting, audit, tax filing and tax advice separate charges?
  • Who prepares and signs the statutory audit?
  • Is banking support an introduction, document review or application-management service?
  • What happens if the bank or payment provider declines the application?
  • Which documents, records and deadlines remain the founder’s responsibility?
