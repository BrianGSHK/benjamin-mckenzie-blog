Limited Company vs Sole Proprietorship: Which Should You Choose?

376|

A practical comparison of Limited Companies and Sole Proprietorships covering liability, taxes, compliance, and costs to help you make the right decision.

377|
378|
379|

381| Aug 11, 2026 · 7 min read 382|
383|

A Limited Company is a separate legal entity that protects your personal assets from business debts, while a Sole Proprietorship is simpler and cheaper but leaves you personally liable for all business obligations. For Hong Kong incorporation details, consult the Companies Registry's official incorporation FAQ. Both structures are legitimate choices, but the right one depends on your business size, risk tolerance, and growth plans. Quick Answer: Choose a Limited Company if you want liability protection, plan to grow, or work with corporate clients. Choose a Sole Proprietorship if you're a freelancer, consultant, or testing a business idea with minimal risk.

Direct Answers

397|

What is the difference between a Hong Kong limited company and sole proprietorship?

398|

A Hong Kong limited company is a separate legal person whose shareholders generally receive limited liability, while a sole proprietorship is operated by one individual without a separate legal identity. A company usually involves more administration, records, annual filings, and audit requirements, whereas a sole proprietorship is simpler but exposes the owner to business liabilities.

399|

Which Hong Kong business structure is better for a small business?

400|

A sole proprietorship may suit a low-risk freelancer or individual testing a simple business because setup and administration are comparatively straightforward. A limited company may be more suitable when liability protection, hiring, investment, corporate contracts, or future expansion matter. The decision should consider risk, tax, compliance, financing, and the owner’s long-term plans.

401|

Can a Hong Kong sole proprietorship become a limited company?

402|

A business owner can establish a Hong Kong limited company and transfer or reorganise the business, but this is not simply an automatic conversion of legal identity. Contracts, assets, employees, licences, bank accounts, tax registrations, and accounting records may need separate treatment. Professional advice is sensible before transferring an operating business.

What Is a Limited Company?

406|

A Limited Company (Ltd) is a business entity that is legally separate from its owners. This means the company itself can own assets, enter contracts, and be sued — protecting your personal assets from business liabilities.

407| 408|

Key Characteristics of a Limited Company:

409|
    410|
  • Limited liability — Shareholders' liability is limited to their unpaid share capital
    • 411|
  • Separate legal entity — The company can own property, open bank accounts, and enter contracts
    • 412|
  • Professional credibility — Looks more professional to clients, banks, and partners
    • 413|
  • Perpetual existence — Continues regardless of ownership changes
    • 414|
  • Tax flexibility — Can retain profits, pay dividends, and plan tax more effectively

What Is a Sole Proprietorship?

418|

A Sole Proprietorship (also called a sole trader or individual business) is a business owned and operated by one person. There is no legal distinction between the owner and the business.

419| 420|

Key Characteristics of a Sole Proprietorship:

421|
    422|
  • Unlimited liability — Owner is personally liable for all business debts
    • 423|
  • Simple structure — No legal formalities, easy to set up and close
    • 424|
  • Direct control — Owner makes all decisions without board approval
    • 425|
  • Lower costs — Minimal registration and compliance costs (see our complete cost breakdown)
    • 426|
  • Personal tax — Business income is taxed as personal income

Liability Protection

494|

The most significant difference is liability protection.

495| 496|

Limited Company

497|

If your Limited Company goes bankrupt or is sued, your personal assets (home, savings, car) are protected. Creditors can only claim against the company's assets, not your personal assets.

498| 499|

Sole Proprietorship

500|

If your business cannot pay its debts, creditors can pursue your personal assets. There is no legal separation between you and your business. This means your home, savings, and other personal assets are at risk.
