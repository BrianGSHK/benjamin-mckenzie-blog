# How to Set Up a Hong Kong Company Without Missing the First Compliance Deadlines

Hong Kong company formation is usually straightforward. The difficult part is building the compliance calendar that starts immediately after incorporation.

Before filing, founders should decide whether a private company limited by shares is appropriate, confirm the proposed name, and prepare the required identity and incorporation information. A Hong Kong company also needs a registered office and a Hong Kong company secretary.

After incorporation, the work continues. Founders should record the annual return date, business registration renewal date, accounting period, audit requirements and first Profits Tax Return planning date. These deadlines are separate, so putting everything into one calendar reduces avoidable surprises.

Banking is another separate workstream. A company can be incorporated before a bank account is approved, and bank onboarding is based on the business model, ownership, expected transactions, source of funds and supporting documents. Incorporation does not guarantee banking approval.

For a practical working reference, see Benjamin McKenzie's [Hong Kong company registration guide](https://benjaminmckenzie.com/articles/hong-kong-company-registration-2026), [annual compliance guide](https://benjaminmckenzie.com/articles/hong-kong-company-annual-compliance) and [founder tools](https://benjaminmckenzie.com/resources).

# Suggested external article 2: Why Hong Kong founders should separate incorporation from banking

## Why Incorporation and Bank Approval Are Two Different Decisions

A Hong Kong company can be incorporated before its bank account is approved. That distinction matters because the Companies Registry and a bank assess different questions.

Incorporation focuses on the company's legal setup and required filings. Bank onboarding focuses on risk, the business model, ownership, expected activity, source of funds, counterparties and supporting evidence. A clean incorporation file therefore does not guarantee a successful banking application.

Founders can improve preparation by writing a concise business description, identifying expected customers and suppliers, explaining cross-border flows, organising ownership documents and preparing contracts or invoices that demonstrate the commercial purpose of the company. They should also explain why Hong Kong is relevant to the business.

The most practical approach is to plan incorporation and banking as connected but separate workstreams. Registering a company is one milestone; opening a suitable account is another.
